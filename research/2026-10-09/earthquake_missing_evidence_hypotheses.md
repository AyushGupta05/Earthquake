# Missing evidence after the completed distribution experiments

2026-10-09. Bounded primary-source and existing-code review. No waveform/metadata arrays were opened, no model code changed, and no jobs launched. These are falsifiable hypotheses, not verified gains or novelty claims.

**Recommendation:** the cheapest next scientific question is whether noise-aware **cross-component geometry** explains held-event magnitude residuals beyond the existing model. Historical same-path references are a more demanding second direction. A magnitude-scale observation model is a third, conditional on independent label anchors. None currently justifies claiming the requested all-1/3/5-second improvement. Keep the physical mechanisms separate from the ongoing score experiment.

## What has already been used

`outputs/earthquake_method_status.md` records failures of temporal updates, future-growth/revision supervision, censored innovations, correlated-noise-only preflights, and the shared-cap contrast model. Static gain/unit/site features remain the strongest completed improvement; the reused validation has only 13 M≥4 events and one M≥5 event. A mechanism cannot be established by optimizing those events again.

`work/repo/research/2026-10-09/phase2/feature_residual.py::prefix_features` has **51** descriptors: 9 amplitude, 12 relative quarter-prefix RMS, 15 normalized FFT powers in five bands, and 15 centroid/entropy/crossing/roughness/crest values. Thus “add spectral shape” is not a new experiment. The 12 native-unit amplitudes are additional features, not the whole baseline. Explicit pre-P noise and cross-component phase/covariance are absent from that function, although the full waveform CNN can learn cross-channel structure implicitly. The earlier `causal_shape_and_scaling_scope.md` has been corrected accordingly; its source-scaling obstruction and universal-onset null remain unchanged.

## 1. Separate amplitude–geometry ambiguity using observed vector motion

**Missing information / mechanism.** Marginal E/N/Z power does not specify their signed covariance, polarization, or phase relation. Two records can have identical per-channel power spectra and different cross-spectra. Directional P motion can constrain the ray direction; an actually detected S arrival adds travel-time information. This may distinguish weak motion caused by geometry from genuinely small source size. It cannot reveal future rupture duration.

**Concrete construction to test.** Form prefix-only three-component covariance and short-lag cross-covariance, with a separately estimated pre-P noise covariance. Use regularized generalized polarization eigenvalues/directions, orientation metadata, and detection reliability. Preserve the physical amplitude branch; whitening must not silently erase amplitude. Predict an explicit distribution over coarse distance/depth nuisance cells from these shape measurements, supervised by TRAIN catalogue geometry. A magnitude conditional for each cell uses the same observed amplitude/shape, then sums over cells:

`q_t(m | x) = Σ_g q_t(g | polarization, observed phase evidence, station geometry) q_t(m | amplitude, shape, g, response)`.

This is one conditional distribution computed from the available prefix, **not** a product of a full-prefix posterior and an allegedly independent likelihood. Geometry labels are training targets only. At 3/5 seconds an S input must be a causally detected arrival with a missing/detection-confidence state; the catalogue S time and future absence of a pick are forbidden inference features. At 1 second, polarization alone constrains direction rather than uniquely determining distance and depth. A geometry head must be allowed to remain broad.

**Closest overlap.** [Zollo et al. (2021), *A Bayesian Method for Real-Time Earthquake Location Using Multiparameter Data*](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020JB020359), already combines P-time differences, amplitude ratios and backazimuth with uncertainty. It uses 0.5-second polarization, 2-second amplitudes and at least two stations; its comparison says backazimuth mainly improves epicentre, not depth. [Lara et al. (2023), E3WS](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/2024-01/010088680.pdf), already estimates magnitude, distance, depth and backazimuth using 140 features and independent regressors from 7 seconds pre-P plus 3 seconds post-P. Joint source estimation is therefore established. The possible contribution is the specific noise-conditioned vector observation and uncertainty-preserving coupling under a strict early clock, not “using geometry” or marginalization itself.

**One cheap falsification.** Freeze a new TRAIN event split before extraction. Compare the existing static/full-prefix representation with (a) added vector/noise features directly, (b) the same features plus ordinary geometry auxiliary supervision, and (c) the explicit uncertain-geometry coupling, with matched capacity/budget. First test whether those features improve cross-fitted distance/depth residuals at all; then whether magnitude gains survive held stations and held spatial blocks. A shuffled-geometry-supervision arm detects geographical-prior regularization. If (a) equals (c), retain a feature improvement, reject the coupling claim. If only catalogue geometry supplied at inference works, reject deployability. No tail claim from one large event.

**Can current data identify it?** Three-component arrays and historical catalogue geometry make a conditional diagnostic plausible, subject to an orientation/pre-P/response audit. That audit was not performed here. A single-station ray cannot uniquely determine range; multiple *currently available* station rays/times help. Any network version must compare with TEAM using exactly the same station mask and deadline, not give each station a full local 1/3/5-second window. This is the first recommendation because the potential missing measurement is explicit and cheap, not because success is likely or established.

## 2. Calibrate path effects using an earlier same-path waveform reference

**Missing information / mechanism.** Static response metadata describes the sensor, and a station intercept describes an average site effect; neither identifies source-to-station path distortion. An earlier nearby earthquake recorded by the same station can supply a measured path reference. Under a shared linear path, a target waveform can be approximated by convolution of the reference with a relative source function. This uses a new historical observation, not a more elaborate prior on the same two peak ratios.

**Concrete construction to test.** Retrieve a small archive of earlier TRAIN reference events using only information available by the target deadline, keep their known magnitude/response and uncertainty, and fit a low-dimensional relative source history by the **time-domain forward relation** `R_t y_target ≈ R_t T(y_reference) r + noise`. `T` is causal convolution and `R_t` retains only observed target samples. Do not divide truncated FFTs and assert exact path cancellation: truncation and convolution do not commute. Feed the resulting fit, unresolved directions, and reconstruction residual to the magnitude distribution alongside the original features. Reference mismatch is estimated from TRAIN pairs. A resolved early relative-source prefix is not the final moment of an ongoing rupture.

**Closest overlap.** This rests directly on [Mueller (1985), *Source pulse enhancement by deconvolution of an empirical Green's function*](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/source-pulse-enhancement-deconvolution-empirical-greens-function), and [Van Houtte et al. (2018), *Improved Model Fitting for the Empirical Green's Function Approach Using Hierarchical Models*](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2017JB014943). The latter already models hierarchical source/EGF/focal-sphere effects and warns about unresolved moments, correlated parameters and truncated-window bias. [Chen et al. (2025), *Effect of Time Window and Spectral Measurement Options on Empirical Green's Function Analysis Using DAS Array and Seismic Stations*](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/3k3zw-9b848), further makes window dependence explicit. A possible distinction is deadline-conditioned forward fitting with causal reference retrieval and a magnitude distribution, not EGF correction or hierarchical uncertainty.

**One cheap falsification.** On a frozen TRAIN event/sequence split, compare a fixed same-path reference against a same-station reference matched in magnitude/SNR but deliberately far in source location. Include reference-label-only and raw-pair discriminative controls. Begin with catalogue-selected close pairs only as a clearly labelled *oracle mechanism diagnostic*, not an EEW result. If close pairs give no incremental effect beyond the reference label and current static/CNN features, stop. If they help, causal retrieval must reproduce it without target catalogue coordinates, target magnitude, future waveforms, or aftershocks that had not occurred yet. A raw-pair model matching forward fitting defeats the physical-operator contribution but may preserve a practical retrieval result.

**Can current data identify it?** Repeated regional recordings may support pairs, but their density and timing are unverified here. Similar location does not ensure identical focal mechanism, radiation, attenuation or source extent. No reference means no additional information. Large ruptures violate point-source path matching; a failed one-second reconstruction may be expected. Thus this ranks behind hypothesis 1 and is not a broad augmentation recommendation.

## 3. Treat heterogeneous magnitude labels as observations, not exact common targets

**Missing information / mechanism.** Sensor families, geography and catalogue magnitude type may be correlated. A single scalar loss can then teach the network a mixture of incompatible scale relations. This is distinct from waveform uncertainty and can affect every prefix length.

**Construction and overlap.** With genuinely paired catalogue labels, model each reported value as `y_k ~ p_k(y | m)` and train a latent physical-magnitude distribution using the integrated observation likelihood. Fix its scale with independent moment-magnitude anchors; reported uncertainty is label uncertainty, not predicted waveform uncertainty. [Grigoratos et al. (2023), *Homogenizing instrumental earthquake catalogs*](https://seismica.library.mcgill.ca/article/view/402), already analyzes conversion uncertainty and high/low-end bias; [Jónasson et al. (2021), *A harmonised instrumental earthquake catalogue for Iceland and the northern Mid-Atlantic Ridge*](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/21/2197/2021/nhess-21-2197-2021.html), already models agency corrections and uncertain reference magnitudes. Learned homogenization alone is not novel.

**Cheap falsification / identifiability.** Fit conversions only on overlapping TRAIN-event labels, then compare fixed label conversion, scale-specific heads, and the latent observation model on held-event common-scale anchors. Also retain original catalogue-label metrics so changing the target cannot manufacture a win. With one label per event and an unconstrained conversion function, latent magnitude is not identified: any monotone change of latent scale can be absorbed by the observation model. Paired independent anchors are not established in the present artifacts. **Defer this candidate rather than invent those data.**

## Why generic station disagreement is not a fourth proposal

[SINE (Meng et al., 2025), *Spatio-temporal deep networks with feature disentangling for advancing earthquake monitoring*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0098300425001244), already combines multi-station graph relationships, picking/location/magnitude tasks and feature disentanglement; [author code](https://github.com/Fan-Chun-Meng/SINE) is released. Its indexed publisher abstract was verified, but its exact early-window/split protocol was not audited here. [Bharadwaj (2024), variational SymAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15613v1), already separates coherent event information from waveform-specific nuisance, scores station informativeness, and generates virtual traces. [Bharadwaj et al. (2025), *Learning Earthquake Sources Using Symmetric Autoencoders*](https://ceas.iisc.ac.in/~geophyinv/publication/bharadwaj-2023-learning-earthquakes/bharadwaj-2023-learning-earthquakes.pdf), DOI10.1785/0120250071, extends this to nearly 30 deep-focus Mw≥6 sources and azimuth-dependent source histories. These are not verified 1/3/5-second magnitude benchmarks, but they defeat broad claims of new source/nuisance disentanglement or learned station reliability. TEAM is already a strong multistation aggregation control.

The defensible next step is a predeclared *information diagnostic* for hypothesis 1. Only if it passes should a complete method be trained. An eventual contribution would require a specific construction outperforming its same-input controls on independently held large events while preserving bulk MAE/MedAE and proper-score quality at each deadline. These proposals do not themselves satisfy that requirement.
