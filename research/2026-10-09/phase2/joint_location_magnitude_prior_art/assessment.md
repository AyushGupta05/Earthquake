# Chile: propagating location uncertainty into a magnitude distribution

Source audit dated 10 October 2026. This is a bounded research assessment, not a preregistration or authorization to fit. No waveforms, checkpoints, prediction artifacts, or Chile held-out outcomes were opened. Existing code, documentation and cached schema were inspected; catalog examples incidentally present in a schema-search result were not used in the design. No production code or AWS resources were changed.

**Recommendation:** retain one conditional-density hypothesis for a small, separately frozen TRAIN experiment: infer a coherent hypocentre distribution from the available multistation prefix, evaluate a shared magnitude decoder at its location hypotheses, and average the resulting normalized magnitude densities. The differentiator from the inspected TEAM-LM code is explicit propagation of **one shared source-location uncertainty into all station distances**. Joint magnitude/location inference and distance marginalization are already established EEW ideas. A useful new implementation would need to beat both auxiliary location supervision and point-location conditioning under the same evidence, capacity and training budget. There is currently no evidence that it improves high magnitudes at any deadline.

## 1. What TEAM-LM actually implements

The cached author source is pinned to commit `8df20877f3a6ef47d3af4d1484ecdb0cdf37e903`. Its shared event embedding feeds separate magnitude and location MLPs, then separate mixture-output layers. The Chile joint configuration specifies five magnitude components and fifteen location components. Training applies separate marginal losses with weights 1 and 0.3. There are no shared component assignments, magnitude–location covariance parameters, or magnitude decoder evaluated at inferred source locations. Shared representation learning can still exploit location-related evidence: separate outputs do **not** establish that the physical variables are conditionally independent. They simply do not specify their joint distribution. [Author model, lines 635–641, 686–695 and 712–725](https://github.com/yetinam/TEAM/blob/8df20877f3a6ef47d3af4d1484ecdb0cdf37e903/models.py#L635), [training loss](https://github.com/yetinam/TEAM/blob/8df20877f3a6ef47d3af4d1484ecdb0cdf37e903/train.py#L295), [paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggab139).

The current local `phase3/team_lm.py` is explicitly magnitude-only. Adding the original auxiliary location task is therefore a mandatory comparator. Its gain must not be attributed to uncertainty propagation. The source's separately weighted, density-floored losses should not be described as a proper joint negative log likelihood.

## 3. A coherent construction, and two mathematical traps

Let \(X_t\) contain only waveform samples available at deadline \(t\), station coordinates and the same availability mask as the comparator. Let \(L\) be one three-dimensional hypocentre. A normalized conditional model can be

\[
q(M,L\mid X_t)=q_\phi(L\mid X_t)\,r_\theta(M\mid L,X_t),\qquad
q(M\mid X_t)=\int r_\theta(M\mid L,X_t)q_\phi(L\mid X_t)\,dL.
\]

For each location hypothesis, compute **all** station distances from that same location. A shared decoder pools prefix station embeddings with the resulting distances, relative geometry, observed amplitude and deadline; it outputs a normalized continuous magnitude density. Positive-weight quadrature or samples produce a normalized mixture. Hypocentre support, quadrature error and location-tail treatment must be checked, rather than dropping distant hypotheses and silently renormalizing. The first implementation should add no independent arrival likelihood: it already receives waveform timing information through its encoder.

Reusing \(X_t\) in these two conditional factors is a valid factorization, not an assumption of independent observations. By contrast, multiplying this result by another waveform-amplitude or arrival likelihood generally reuses evidence. Similarly, never multiply the 1-, 3- and 5-second posteriors as though their nested prefixes were independent. Recompute the conditional prediction from each available prefix; a future recurrent version would need an explicit conditional model of genuinely new observations.

**Trap 1: covariance alone.** For an ordinary joint Gaussian mixture,

\[
q(M,L\mid X)=\sum_k\pi_k\,\mathcal N_4([M,L];\mu_k,\Sigma_k),
\quad q(M\mid X)=\sum_k\pi_k\,\mathcal N(M;\mu_{M,k},\Sigma_{MM,k}).
\]

Magnitude–location cross-covariances disappear on marginalization. They can alter learning through joint supervision, but merely introducing them does not analytically correct the magnitude marginal. The proposed test instead exposes a nonlinear distance-dependent decoder to coherent location hypotheses and compares integration against a point hypothesis.

**Trap 2: uncertainty is not an upward adjustment.** Even a simple correction involving \(\log R\) has \(E[\log R]\leq\log E[R]\); other geometries and posterior correlations complicate the sign. Wider location uncertainty can broaden or lower a magnitude estimate. It cannot be sold as a guaranteed correction for high-magnitude underestimation. Early rupture incompleteness, path/site effects and sensor response can remain confounded after hypocentral distance is known.

Paired TRAIN catalog locations may supervise the joint model. Proper joint NLL, \(-\log q_\phi(L^*\mid X)-\log r_\theta(M^*\mid L^*,X)\), is valid but trains the conditional decoder at catalog locations; it does not by itself guarantee reliable behavior over imperfect predicted-location hypotheses. A marginal magnitude NLL term can target deployed predictions. If combined, call the objective a **composite proper score**, state the fixed weights, and do not claim that marginal calibration uniquely identifies the physical joint coupling. Match this objective and its supervision controls before fitting. Source magnitude oversampling/label smoothing also changes the training distribution; a normalized density is not automatically a calibrated natural-frequency posterior.

## 4. Chile causal feasibility and remaining prerequisites

The existing schema audit records `LAT`, `LON`, `DEPTH` as float64 catalog fields. These are available as **training labels**, but are absent from the current five-column metadata CSV used by the AWS runner. A separately reviewed TRAIN-only sidecar would be needed. Only pre-existing schema evidence was used; no new label export or data-driven diagnostic was performed.

The documented cache contains synchronous 100 Hz ZNE velocity, station latitude/longitude/depth, and a network-first-P reference. Strict 1/3/5-second inputs end at exclusive indices 600/800/1000. It contains no per-station P-pick arrays; continuous prearrival noise is retained. The mechanism is implementable without inventing picks or giving catalog location/origin time to the model. Sensor sensitivity is corrected, but full instrument response is not removed. [Local audit](../repo/research/2026-10-09/phase3/CHILE_DATA_AUDIT.md), [official dataset](https://doi.org/10.5880/GFZ.2.4.2021.002).

The published time origin remains a benchmark alignment, not a demonstrated online detector. Do not subtract catalog `TIME` to provide origin time, calculate oracle per-station arrivals, select stations by true event distance, or align each trace using future picks. No-trigger evidence is informative only when a causal detector's sensitivity and station availability are modeled; a silent prefix is not proof that P has not arrived. A travel-time extension would need causal pick uncertainty and an inferred/marginalized origin time, or relative travel times, with all look-ahead excluded.

At one second, many stations can still contain noise. Additional stations help only when actual moveout, polarization or amplitude-pattern evidence constrains location; the geometry distribution may otherwise mainly reproduce regional seismicity. A hypocentre also does not determine finite-rupture geometry. Therefore this is an amplitude/path ambiguity hypothesis, not a solution to rupture predictability. The failed INSTANCE polarization diagnostic does not settle a multistation timing hypothesis, but it prevents claiming that the added geometry information is already empirically established.

## 5. Minimum decisive comparison before substantial investment

Freeze a fresh event-separated partition within official TRAIN, location support, objective, quadrature budget and exactly matched sampling before reading outcomes. Keep DEV/TEST sealed. Use the same proper continuous magnitude score, backbone initialization, station masks, cutoff distribution, update budget and at least two paired seeds for:

1. **Magnitude-only control.** The continuous baseline, with any numerical score change applied consistently.
2. **Auxiliary-location control.** Shared encoder and separate magnitude/location outputs, source-like supervision, with matched head capacity. This isolates extra labels/representation learning.
3. **Point-geometry control.** The same location-conditioned decoder architecture using a fixed point summary of the predicted location distribution, declared in advance.
4. **Integrated geometry.** The same decoder evaluated across the coherent location distribution. Also evaluate this fitted model with its distribution collapsed to the declared point: this no-refit contrast isolates integration from retraining differences, while arm 3 supplies the separately optimized point comparator.

All arms must retain identical population/exposure and magnitude support. Match auxiliary losses and parameter counts in the relevant contrasts; report residual compute differences rather than pretending extra decoder evaluations are free. Any training-only catalog-location “oracle” ablation must be clearly separate from deployable results. Spatial holdouts are needed later to distinguish propagation of physical distance uncertainty from memorized regional magnitude–location priors.

Before a large run, require two cheap falsification checks on the separately frozen TRAIN holdout: calibrated location uncertainty must predict nontrivial **magnitude sensitivity to plausible geometry**, and integrated predictions must improve on both their collapsed version and the separately fitted point control without just inflating intervals. If the decoder ignores location, q-location is overconfident/wrong, or integration only increases spread without a proper-score gain, reject the mechanism.

For a later investment gate, predeclare tail event-macro improvement and bulk MAE/MedAE noninferiority at all three deadlines, for both posterior means and medians, together with NLL/CRPS and coverage. Do not pick whichever summary or deadline wins. Current TRAIN has only 22 MA≥5.5 events in the recorded audit; its existing calibration portion has two. This is insufficient to establish a general rare-large-earthquake claim. A positive cheap diagnostic would justify a larger frozen comparison, not a foundational-paper or superiority claim.

**Decision:** scientifically defensible as a narrowly specified multistation architecture hypothesis; not yet earned as the next expensive run. The nearest-prior-art distinction is explicit coherent geometry integration versus TEAM's separate marginals and point-location neural pipelines. The decisive evidence is improvement over matched auxiliary-supervision and point-geometry controls, including one-second benefit, not the existence of a joint PDF.
