# Catalog availability evidence, 11 October 2026

This is a documentation-only prerequisite audit. No earthquake records, catalog files, revision payloads, data artifacts, waveforms, models or private files were requested. The sources establish useful interfaces and one daily-version archive lead. They do not establish a complete New Zealand catalog containing the exact bytes received before real 1/3/5-second EEW deadlines.

The source pins below are official URLs, named sections and identifiable editions/manuscripts. Mutable web pages were observed on 11 October 2026; their original bytes were not downloaded or SHA-verified. A stable URL or version identifier is not a proof of immutable bytes, historical publication, or consumer receipt. Public packet hashes can be recorded by the parent separately.

## Close primary prior art

**bEPIC capsule (under 160 words).** Williamson, Lux and Allen's accepted author manuscript, published as *Improving Out of Network Earthquake Locations Using Prior Seismicity for Use in Earthquake Early Warning* (BSSA, 2023), adds a spatial seismicity prior to EPIC's arrival-time location grid; magnitude estimates use the resulting location and P-wave displacement. Its ComCat background contains M≥3 events and its replay excludes earthquakes whose rupture dates reach the target time. The manuscript explicitly assumes access to an updated seismicity catalog and identifies the ComCat query access date. That design is close prior art for history-assisted EEW and can affect magnitude through location. Occurrence-time truncation does not by itself prove that the historical estimates were published or received before each replay deadline. This audit does not reproduce its data or performance. [Accepted author manuscript](https://escholarship.org/content/qt626926bz/qt626926bz_noSplash_b55a5947d29c9eb87844572059dfb5cd.pdf), pp. 4–11 and Data and Resources; stable repository identifier `qt626926bz`, filename labels accepted manuscript.

Yin, Andrews and Heaton's primary 2017 meeting abstract already combines three-component single-station waveforms and an ETAS forecast in a Bayesian detector. This is detection prior art, not a demonstrated magnitude-density result or a historical-receipt archive. [JpGU–AGU 2017 proceedings](https://www2.jpgu.org/meeting/2017/PDF2017/S-CG72_O.pdf), SCG72-07, PDF page 8. The 2018 BSSA article is cited by bEPIC but was not independently read in this bounded audit.

The earlier MAGNET source packet covers magnitude densities from catalog history and unknown current query coordinates. Virtual Seismologist already provides Bayesian EEW prior/likelihood context. Accordingly, combining a catalog prior and waveforms cannot itself be claimed novel. The unresolved contribution would have to be a specific, correctly conditioned and independently validated improvement with defensible availability evidence, rather than the existence of Bayesian fusion. [MAGNET primary journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-48563-7), [Virtual Seismologist primary paper](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~heatont/papers/VSmethod_cua_heaton%20%282%29.pdf); these are cross-references to the separately completed source packet, not new reads here.
