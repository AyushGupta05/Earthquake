# Physical clipping as an early magnitude observation

Source-only audit, 11 October 2026 (London). Read the completed censoring, physical-prefix and instrument scientific reports and the frozen GeoNet feasibility protocol before researching primary papers and official documentation. No raw data, per-target inventories/picks, saved prediction files, new acquisition, implementation, fit, AWS action or reviewer submission was used. The GeoNet protocol is unchanged.

**Decision: zero new diagnostics and zero fits are earned now.** Known physical saturation is a distinct observation operator from the completed scalar amplitude-growth pilot. It can prevent falsely treating a hardware ceiling as the actual motion. But clipping-aware magnitude likelihoods, waveform restoration and switching to co-located strong-motion sensors already have substantial prior art. Our inspected provenance does not establish epoch-specific hardware rails, nonlinear sensor behavior and health at the requested deadlines. The already frozen prefix screen can report candidates; it cannot establish this mechanism.

## Distinctions that matter

| Mechanism | What is measured or lost | What this audit concludes |
|---|---|---|
| Completed censored amplitude innovation | `G=max(0,Z)` compresses the signed change in an observed local peak between 1→3→5 seconds. The threshold is a statistical summary boundary, not a hardware rail. | [The pilot](../../earthquake_censored_results.md) lost to stronger full-prefix inference and failed its mechanism controls. Its rejection is neither evidence for nor a direct test of physical clipping. |
| Gain/unit correction or coherent gain augmentation | Correctly scaling counts and gain preserves the physical interpretation of the same observation while within the linear range. | An invertible scale cannot reconstruct amplitude already discarded by saturation. Changing gain does not automatically identify the real acquisition system's range. |
| Hardware clipping | Sensor mass/feedback, analogue electronics, preamplifier or ADC may leave their valid linear range. A verified ADC rail can indicate a bound on its input instead of an exact value. | A potentially useful observation-model correction, with known prior art. Different clipping stages have different bounds and recovery behavior; a single hard-clamp model is not universally valid. |
| Early source/magnitude saturation | Several final magnitudes can produce similar limited-time motion, even with a perfectly healthy unsaturated instrument. | Hardware treatment cannot create unavailable final-rupture information. [The physical-prefix preflight](../../earthquake_physical_prefix_preflight.md) preserves an explicit causal indistinguishability example; its covariance improvement did not preserve rare-event error. |

The [instrument scientific audit](../../earthquake_instrument_scientific_audit.md) found a useful exploratory metadata gain, strongly concentrated in HN recordings. Its fields describe scalar sensitivity, units, reference frequency, rate, family and site. They do not supply verified sensor/ADC saturation thresholds. A family effect alone does not establish clipping: families also differ in units, sites, path, selection and historical configuration.

## Hardware documentation: required information is not the file type

The following are examples of what must be joined to a deployed channel and epoch, not asserted instrument identities for our data.

- [Nanometrics Centaur CTR4 specification](https://nanometrics.ca/hubfs/Downloads/Data%20Sheets/Centaur%20DigitalRecorder%20Data%20Sheet_1001.01.27.pdf?hsLang=en-ca) lists configurable input ranges, analogue gain and digital gain, and selectable output bit depth. A decoded int32 sample or “24-bit instrument” is therefore insufficient to infer the delivered count ceiling.
- [Güralp DM24 documentation](https://www.guralp.com/documents/html/MAN-D24-0004/s6.html) describes channel-specific gain settings reported in its status stream. [Manufacturer sensor documentation](https://www.guralp.com/documents/MAN-030-0001.pdf) separately specifies sensor full scale and sensitivity; [the Minimus/integrated-instrument manual](https://london.guralp.com/documents/MAN-DIG-0001.pdf) describes frequency-dependent clip level and long-period-corner effects. Sensor range and ADC range must not be conflated.
- [FDSN StationXML reference](https://docs.fdsn.org/projects/stationxml/en/latest/reference.html) describes response stages, scalar sensitivity and optional sensor/logger equipment fields. Passing a linear-response consistency check does not certify nonlinear limits, deployed analogue gain, mass position or operational health. A scalar count-to-physical-unit sensitivity is not a universal physical clipping threshold.
- [FDSN miniSEED headers](https://docs.fdsn.org/projects/miniseed3/en/latest/appendix.html) and [EarthScope libmseed documentation](https://earthscope.github.io/libmseed/porting-guide.html) distinguish amplifier saturation from digitizer clipping flags. Those are producer-reported detections, not automatic sample-level truth. Absence of a flag is not independently verified absence of clipping. A record-wide flag from a record extending beyond the deadline cannot establish when the detection became available or whether it occurred within the prefix; this is a causal-use limitation, not a claim the specification prohibits such records.
- [GeoNet naming conventions](https://www.geonet.org.nz/data/supplementary/channels) describe weak/strong-motion location conventions and state-of-health channels. A naming convention is not proof of co-location, identical ground coupling, simultaneous receipt or historical range settings for a given pair.

The [frozen GeoNet protocol](../geonet_waveform_feasibility/PROTOCOL_v1.md) currently requests one literal station/location/channel prefix from retrospective picks. It does not acquire a second co-located sensor family, ADC configuration history or extra health streams. The requests and denominators must remain unchanged. Its repeated prefix-extreme and flatline screen explicitly returns **clipping candidates**, and says a clean screen cannot prove physical unclipping or ADC full scale. Response stages and a valid evalresp value prove only the stated metadata checks.

## Own scalar observation derivation

This is an invented mathematical audit, not a fitted seismological law. Let `U` be the healthy sensor-chain output immediately before a known ideal hard clipping operator, already in the appropriate electronic units. If the upper observation is censored at `c`, an upper-rail observation has likelihood

`P(U >= c | source state, response, noise, initial state)`.

An interior quantized ADC code instead has the probability of its corresponding input bin. In a continuous idealization, interior observations use a density and the rails have probability masses; all are part of one normalized mixed observation law. A detector's clip flag, when available, must be incorporated according to its actual threshold semantics. Do not multiply a density evaluated at `c` by a censoring mass as though they were independent observations.

For a real trace, filtering, decimation, orientation rotation, analogue saturation and recovery can make the recorded output a different nonlinear operator. The rail bound is on the input to the verified clipping stage, not automatically on instantaneous ground velocity, displacement or magnitude. Noise and initial-state uncertainty remain part of the source likelihood. Correlated clipped samples require a joint/conditional observation law; repeated samples or nested prefix maxima cannot be counted as independent new tail events.

Known physical clipping removes an upper equality assumption; it does not reveal how far past the ceiling the signal went. Two large-source hypotheses with identical probabilities of the observed saturation pattern remain indistinguishable. An on-scale co-located sensor may add actual amplitude information, if its response, coupling, health and receipt timing are verified and supplied to every comparator.

## Invented scalar counterexample

The calculations in [counterexample.json](counterexample.json) use exact fractions and no real waveforms. The source labels “small” and “large” are hypothetical classes with equal priors. The latent discrete sensor output takes four values:

| `U` | Small-class probability | Large-class probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1/10 | 1/20 |
| 1 | 3/10 | 1/20 |
| 2 | 1/2 | 1/20 |
| 4 | 1/10 | 17/20 |

The invented observation is `Y=min(U,c)`. For a verified `c=2`, `Y=2` represents `U>=2`: likelihoods are 3/5 and 9/10 and the posterior large-class probability is **3/5**. Incorrectly treating the same value as exact `U=2` gives **1/11**. The censoring operator can therefore prevent a specific artificial tail suppression.

For an unknown ADC setting, suppose `c=2` or `c=8` each has independent probability 1/2. The same observed `Y=2` then has likelihoods 11/20 and 19/40, giving **19/41**, not 3/5. For an unknown-health alternative with only 1/10 probability of the healthy `c=2` channel and 9/10 probability of a stuck output fixed at 2 independently of source, the correct posterior is **33/65**, close to the prior. These arbitrary probabilities demonstrate dependence on verified observation state, not calibrated prevalence in a network.

Two invented eight-sample prefixes are numerically identical: input 4 with range 2 yields eight clipped 2s; input 2 with range 8 yields eight on-scale 2s. Both pass the same repeated-extreme screen. A faulty channel stuck at 2 is another identical prefix. Thus the sample pattern alone cannot prove full-scale clipping. Conversely, upstream analogue clipping followed by digital filtering need not yield repeated exact extrema, so failure of that screen is not proof of absence.

With `U=g*A`, known gain `g=1`, `A=4` and `c=2`, correcting the recorded value by gain returns 2, not the discarded 4. Coherently rescaling the already recorded value and sensitivity preserves this same corrected value; it cannot undo the noninjective clamp. This is different from arguing that coherent gain augmentation itself is wrong.

## Gate for even a future no-fit diagnostic

The purpose of a no-fit diagnostic would be to establish that **verified early hardware clipping exists and affects available amplitude information**, not to estimate a new model's error or prove a paper contribution. Before proposing new raw reads/acquisition, require all of the following:

1. **Deployed observation operator:** a uniquely joined sensor, preamplifier and digitizer model/configuration/epoch; documented physical range and ADC input range; actual analogue/digital gain, output coding/bin boundaries, offsets, filters/decimation and rotation. Resolve which stage saturates and its documented recovery behavior. Generic manuals or a learned maximum cannot replace the channel-specific join. Preserve unknown states rather than imputing a rail.
2. **Independent state evidence:** valid calibration/health or reliable vendor/operator clipping indications with timestamps and scope through the deadline. Verify how flags are generated and whether they survive archival repacking. If flags are only record-wide and their timing straddles a deadline, retain “unknown within prefix.” Document relevant mass-position, supply, centering and fault states when needed to distinguish motion from overload/failure.
3. **Correct earliest observation:** freeze one common benchmark clock and strict 1/3/5-second prefixes before outcomes. For an operational claim, use receipt watermarks and causal picker/association state; retrospective annotated P plus sample time permits only an explicitly named characterization diagnostic. Avoid future extrema, whole-record clipping classifiers, full-record recovery, later flags and eventual catalog geometry in inference inputs.
4. **Frozen sample and reference:** retain all preselected event/station/deadline denominators. Document genuinely co-located, contemporaneous on-scale reference sensors when available, including response/units/orientation, coupling and timing. Match the same ground-motion quantity through known causal operators rather than directly comparing velocity counts with acceleration counts. Future waveform suffixes may not validate an earlier inference input; any later offline corroboration must be separately labeled.
5. **Prespecified no-fit endpoints:** report the number of events and station prefixes with verified clipping, candidates, verified on-scale reference, missing operator and unknown health; clipping onset relative to each deadline; physical sensor family; and paired available-amplitude discrepancies with uncertainty. High final magnitude and a late clipped S wave do not demonstrate early P-window clipping. Require a positive verified affected cohort before considering any model proposal.

Even a successful diagnostic would earn only a controlled comparison against established clipping exclusion/VS switching, the same all-sensor waveform/status inputs, and a normalized censoring likelihood with matched nuisance treatment. Reconstruction adds assumptions; a second sensor adds observations; a more capable model adds capacity. They must be separated. Independent event/station evaluation, proper-score, bulk-error and worst-error guards remain necessary. No such diagnostic or comparison is performed or authorized by this audit.

Our current reports and frozen feasibility preparation do not establish the first two prerequisites. The short prefix screen remains useful for acquisition quality control within its stated scope. **Stop this proposed mechanism here: no expanded sensor acquisition, diagnostic implementation or fit is earned, and no novelty or improvement claim follows from the sketch.**
