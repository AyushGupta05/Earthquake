# Two bounded GeoNet Mw experiments

**Recommendation: test a spectral encoder as an engineering baseline, then test uncertain geometry only if the additional currently observed arrival times help.** Neither is a demonstrated improvement or a novelty claim. The second has the more specific potential contribution: integrating a learned source-location distribution into an early magnitude density rather than substituting one inferred distance. No code, acquisition, fit, reviewer submission, AWS action or shared-file edit was performed for this shortlist.

## Evidence that determines the shortlist

The completed static response/site model improves bulk, median, high-magnitude and CVaR errors at 1/3/5 seconds on reused INSTANCE validation. Gain/unit fields explain much of that gain; station reuse and only 13 M≥4 validation events limit interpretation. Early FiLM response conditioning and coherent gain augmentation subsequently fail their fixed independent panels; sensitivity-normalized amplitude also trades held-station tail error against bulk error. Keep a strong **late-metadata** control, rather than reopening an invariance loss. See [static replication](../../earthquake_instrument_full_results.md), [factor controls](../../earthquake_instrument_factor_results.md), and [distribution negatives](../../earthquake_distribution_pilots.md).

The existing features already include amplitude, quarter-prefix RMS, FFT powers and spectral shape. The completed polarization probe tests per-record noise/cross-component features and fails its magnitude/distance/depth criteria; it is not a test of multiple stations' observed P-time differences. Sequential logits/entropy, future-growth/revision targets, censored amplitude updates and proper-score-only changes have failed their stated objectives. They are not candidate ingredients here. See [method ledger](../../earthquake_method_status.md) and [polarization results](../../earthquake_polarization_results.md).

The strongest directly relevant published density baseline is TEAM-LM: variable-station attention, prefix amplitude preservation and a five-Gaussian magnitude head; magnitude and location are trained separately. FME supplies a strong spectral architecture comparison but uses a scalar head, distance corrections and a fourth-arrival clock. DHLnet shows the importance of distance inputs and also uses predicted-distance inference. These establish the architecture and geometry ingredients; they do not supply a matched GeoNet Mw result. [TEAM-LM primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.02010), [FME](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44195-026-00121-4), [DHLnet](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023EA003363).

## Shared engineering contract

Both experiments use one event-weighted model trained at the three prespecified horizons, sampled uniformly across 1/3/5 seconds, and the same event/sequence/station partitions, input policy and optimizer budget. They estimate the pinned CMT **Mw** target, not Chile MA. Train the GeoNet baseline on its own TRAIN cohort; a frozen-backbone pilot refers to that TRAIN-built encoder, not an unadapted Chile checkpoint. Any later transfer initialization must be matched across arms with its exposure documented. The source frame reaches Mw8.0; use the same five-component Gaussian-mixture magnitude head in every arm, with uncapped means and positive scales. Gaussians have unbounded support. Evaluate stable normalized log density without a density floor, plus CRPS. Do not carry over the old 0..6.5/6.55 PMF or multiply probabilities above six by ten.

Use the fixed common T0: earliest accepted associated NZ P. Reveal only samples and accepted picks strictly before T0+t. Every station has that same global endpoint; later picks do not earn a full station-local t seconds. Preserve a fixed available preamble, channel identity, response/unit/family/site metadata and missingness. Use identical causal preprocessing and any rate conversion in all arms. An FFT over a completely available prefix is permissible for its terminal forecast; transforming a full future record and then cropping is not. Mask unknown padding, and verify that changing arbitrary future suffixes leaves each earlier prediction exactly unchanged.

Counts-based supervised controls need valid timestamps/channels and declared preprocessing, rather than a claim of exact physical deconvolution. Give all arms the same audited late response/site features; where sensitivity-only summaries are supplied, label them as such and supply them to every arm. Exact response correction is a separate claim. No catalogue hypocentre, distance, depth, origin time, final station count or later annotation enters deployment features. This remains an annotation-conditioned retrospective benchmark, not an operational picker/telemetry proof.

### 1. Spectral station encoder inside TEAM-LM

**Concrete change.** Replace only TEAM-LM's per-station convolutional feature extractor with a small FME-inspired Fourier branch. A starting design is two spectral/residual blocks at width32, retaining a pointwise path and the first32 temporal modes; append a prefix mask and elapsed duration. Process each station separately, then use the unchanged permutation-invariant station attention and magnitude head. Keep the same normalized waveform plus separately preserved prefix log-amplitude contract as the CNN control. Retain the late response/site branch. This hybrid intentionally differs from FME's fixed ten-station spatial processing and LSTM; it is an adaptation, not a reproduction.

**Hypothesis.** A learned spectral mixing operator may use available low-frequency shape more efficiently than the convolutional encoder, while retaining amplitude and heterogeneous sensor context. It adds a representation, not a new observation. It does not reconstruct an unresolved final source corner frequency from one second or remove sensor response by itself.

**Matched falsification.** Compare CNN and Fourier encoders with the same magnitude density, amplitude channel, metadata, station masks, training clocks and updates; match trainable parameter count within 5% using projection width. Add a fixed prefix FFT-power branch to the CNN as the conventional representation control, using the same input slots and projection budget. If that branch matches the Fourier model, retain an ordinary spectral-feature gain and reject an operator-specific contribution. If Fourier improves only NLL, or its tail gain accompanies worse bulk/MedAE/CVaR, it fails the project objective. Do not compare its density mean against another model's tuned median.

**Targets and compute.** Only the TRAIN CMT Mw labels are needed; standardizers are TRAIN-fitted, never full-record amplitude-fitted. Cache validated prefixes once. Three arms, one shared model across deadlines, two seeds: six fits, rather than separate horizon architectures or a foundation-model sweep. First perform a small TRAIN-internal sequence-held pilot with one seed and fixed updates; repeat the two-seed experiment only if the fixed objective survives. That pilot is an engineering screen, not a published-score comparison; an undertrained null cannot establish superiority. No wall-time or cost estimate is asserted before profiling.

**Likely failure.** Sensor transfer functions or prefix edge effects dominate its learned low-frequency modes; one second contains insufficient source-duration information. Greater flexibility may simply memorize station/region amplitude statistics. Report held-station and calendar results, not just a favorable seen-station result.

**Overlap.** FME already uses spectral convolution for magnitude, and TEAM-LM already combines waveform shape with amplitude and outputs distributions. The proposed hybrid's merit must be incremental accuracy under the strict clock, not a claim that Fourier magnitude inference or Gaussian-mixture EEW is new. FME's published fourth-arrival ML scores cannot be compared directly with these first-arrival Mw scores. [FME primary methodology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44195-026-00121-4).

### 2. Integrate an inferred geometry distribution into the magnitude density

**Distinct available observable.** For every currently accepted P arrival, expose its observed time relative to T0 and that station's coordinates, along with masks and horizon. Pairwise differences cancel unknown origin time. With one arrival there is no timing difference and the geometry prediction must be allowed to remain broad. Do not interpret unpicked stations as negative arrival evidence: the frozen archive does not establish live station health or delivery. This does not reuse the failed single-record polarization descriptor hypothesis.

**Concrete change.** Fit a small set encoder and a three-component full-covariance location mixture `q(g | observed times, station coordinates, t)`, where g is local horizontal coordinates and depth. TRAIN preferred-origin geometry is auxiliary supervision only. Form station distances from samples of this **inferred** distribution, never the target catalogue location at inference. Supply each sampled distance vector to the same magnitude conditional `q(m | waveform embedding, metadata, inferred geometry, t)`, and average its normalized five-Gaussian density:

`q_t(m) = sum_k w_k q_t(m | h_t, R, g_k)`, with positive weights summing to one.

Use a fixed small quadrature/sample set (initially16 geometry points) and report numerical stability when doubled. Optimize the log of the integrated magnitude density, not an average of losses that pretends every sampled hypocentre is the true source. The changed coupling is explicit: one sampled source generates a correlated distance vector for all currently observed stations; that vector interacts with their amplitude/response embeddings before the magnitude head. Point geometry collapses this relation once, while direct fusion can approximate it without an imposed integration. This is an inductive-bias test with TRAIN geometry supervision, not information created by integration. Conditional heads are not established physical likelihoods merely because their marginal density scores well.

This remains one full-prefix density; it does not multiply earlier and later posteriors or assume a new block's amplitude is independent of the earlier waveform. Timing-only geometry weights approximate total conditioning when waveform shape/amplitude also informs location: exact conditioning would require the corresponding waveform-conditioned location distribution and a correctly conditional magnitude head. The proposed normalized mixture is an engineering model, not an exact Bayes or verified physical propagation claim.

**Train-only construction.** Use two event/sequence folds to generate out-of-fold geometry predictions for all magnitude-head TRAIN rows, then refit the geometry model on TRAIN before DEV/TEST. Freeze its coordinates, normalizers and mixture settings from TRAIN. Include single-arrival TRAIN cases in the locator's likelihood training so its broad conditional prior is learned rather than fabricated; report its one-arrival held-TRAIN coverage. Missing or invalid geometry labels skip the auxiliary loss, without silently dropping valid Mw examples; the magnitude control retains the same examples and a declared geometry-missing fallback. No predicted distance is computed from its own TRAIN catalogue target, and no future waveform or Mw label is fed into the geometry predictor. A timing-free geometry control detects learning a station/geographical prior alone.

**Matched falsification.** Begin with a cheap frozen-backbone head experiment. All arms receive exactly the same waveform embedding, metadata and current P times: (a) ordinary direct density plus the geometry network's distribution parameters, (b) the same conditional head at the inferred geometry mean, and (c) the integrated density above. Also supply the point/direct controls with each station's inferred `E[log R]`, rather than comparing only with `log(E[R])`. Equalize head parameters and updates. Fit the geometry network once per fold and share it; do not grant the integrated arm a stronger locator. If direct timing/geometry-summary fusion matches (c), the useful result is information fusion, not marginalization. If the log-distance expectation or point geometry matches (c), uncertainty integration has no demonstrated point-error benefit. Report a timing-free arm diagnostically on TRAIN only; no extra TEST selection. A known catalogue-distance oracle can diagnose the maximum potential geometry gain on TRAIN, but earns no deployment result.

**Linear null, without a fit.** For conditional mean `E[M|a,R]=c(a)+b log10(R)` and waveform-independent location weights, the integrated mean is exactly `c(a)+b E[log10(R)]`. This is a matched mixture-mean control; integration cannot improve its mean decision. Invented equal weights at R=10 and100, c=4 and b=1 give conditional means5 and6, integrated mean5.5, and the same plug-in result at E[log10 R]=1.5. Plugging E[R]=55 instead gives5.74036. A gain over that last value alone is a nonlinear/Jensen correction, not evidence for uncertainty integration. The mixture can still improve density scores while the mean errors stay identical, which is insufficient for the requested tail objective. The nonlinear neural coupling must beat this appropriate null.

**Compute and expected failure.** The added locator and conditional heads can be small CPU fits over cached features; the waveform encoder need not be retrained for the first falsification. Reuse the strongest prespecified encoder, rather than selecting an encoder after seeing this experiment's tail scores. Sparse one-second arrivals may make distance unidentified; a learned prior can underestimate unfamiliar offshore/deep large events. Marginalization may widen densities without moving the decision appropriately. Hypocentral distance also cannot stand in for evolving rupture distance during a large finite fault. Any gain appearing only at 3/5 seconds fails the all-deadline objective.

**Closest prior art.** Zollo et al. already infer evolving location uncertainty from differential P times, amplitude ratios and backazimuth. Virtual Seismologist already models joint magnitude/location uncertainty, station site corrections and sparse early observations; it explicitly discusses point-source limitations for long ruptures. Wu et al. already use Bayesian parameter inference for concurrent events. Thus neither joint inference, distance uncertainty, site correction nor marginalization is new. The narrow candidate contribution is the tested coupling of a causal learned geometry distribution to a strong neural magnitude density under identical input, decision and clock controls. [Zollo primary paper](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020JB020359), [VS author chapter](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~heatont/papers/VSmethod_cua_heaton%20%282%29.pdf), [Wu primary article record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/0cdjs-00z18).

## Decision rule, rather than a new architecture grid

Freeze a TRAIN-internal sequence-held pilot and one primary point decision, preferably the mixture mean for continuity with TEAM-LM. Report posterior-median decisions secondarily for every arm. Use event MAE/MedAE, Mw≥5.5 and ≥6 MAE, signed tail bias, all-event CVaR95/P95/max, and proper scores. Select one candidate only if both seeds improve tail error at every deadline and preserve bulk/median/worst-error within prespecified tolerances. TRAIN-only paired residuals determine achievable precision; no tolerance is selected from DEV/TEST forecasts. Calibration or wider tail probability alone does not satisfy this rule.

The frozen GeoNet metadata phase remains unchanged. The full pinned frame's candidate calendar TEST contains at most49/19/5 events above Mw5.5/6/7, before sequence and waveform exclusions. This prevents a strong ≥7 aggregate claim from five cases. The 346+149 metadata screen contains at most169 candidate TEST events (120 sampled lower-M plus49 tails), less than the proposed200 available-TEST planning floor even before attrition. It screens a future larger acquisition; it cannot itself become that benchmark without a separate prospective design. A useful engineering experiment can proceed on a defined usable cohort without demanding exact full-response physics; uncertainty/physical-mechanism attribution still needs the matched controls above. This shortlist proposes no paid work or data requests now.
