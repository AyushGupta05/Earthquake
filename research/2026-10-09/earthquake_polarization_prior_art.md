# Noise-relative polarization and geometry distributions: overlap audit

**10 October 2026. Research-only; no new extraction, fitting, or model changes.**

The current evidence does **not** justify extending the polarization probe into a GPU-trained geometry distribution model. The fixed TRAIN-internal probe failed its gate in every deadline/station panel. Moreover, joint magnitude–distance inference, uncertainty-weighted polarization location, and site-conditioned early-warning models all have direct prior art. A narrower construction could still be useful, but only after a new, separately justified observation-quality test; this memo is not a novelty or performance claim.

## One narrower mechanism, parked rather than authorized

If independent acquisition-response work eventually identifies a measurable information gap, the specific construction worth revisiting is a **response-conditioned distribution over axial polarization, carried through one joint magnitude–geometry predictor**. The proposed difference from a point-axis feature is preserving ambiguity between plausible ray axes, including the sign ambiguity and weak-eigenvalue-separation cases, while explicitly representing which components/frequencies the acquisition operator can observe. This is an engineering adaptation of established inverse-problem ideas; a bounded search did not verify an exact matching EEW magnitude implementation, which is not proof of novelty.

Its observation model would start with a common physical wavefield and a known forward operator, for example

\[
y_{c,0:t}=R_t H_c\{a\,u_c\,s_{0:t}\}+n_{c,0:t},\qquad u\equiv -u.
\]

Here H includes the usable digital response, R_t restricts all processing to the current deadline, a/s are unknown amplitude/shape, and pre-P observations inform noise. An orientation distribution could then condition the **single normalized** predictor

\[
q_t(M)=\int q_\theta(M,g,u\mid y_{\le t},n_{\rm pre},H)\,dg\,du.
\]

This must not be implemented as multiplying a polarization likelihood into a full-prefix magnitude network which already consumed the same waveform. At3/5s the model either recomputes from the available prefix or uses a proven conditional innovation factorization. An ordinary covariance/Wishart likelihood with samples treated as independent is inappropriate without addressing temporal correlation from waveform physics and filtering. Full instrument response still does not identify unknown site/path transfer, and single-station direction does not identify range or depth. These ambiguities must remain in the output rather than be hidden by catalogue priors.

The proposed modification is **not currently ready for training**. First, on an independently frozen TRAIN-internal paired-sensor subset, compare acquisition-adjusted axial agreement for simultaneous same-event/same-location recordings. Fix co-location, clock alignment, frequency acceptance and missing-response rules before reading outcomes. Compare scalar sensitivity only, known full response, and an intentionally wrong response; preserve the same pairs. This measures whether a usable response correction exists, not whether magnitude improved. Pairing does not remove differing installation/site effects; failure to improve agreement ends this branch. The required raw histories and complete responses for this paired test have not been established by this memo.

Only if that independent gate passes should a separately preregistered predictor compare: direct same-feature magnitude distribution; point-geometry auxiliary supervision; geometry/axis mixture with fixed uncertainty; and the proposed observation-dependent uncertainty. Capacity, training population and objectives must match; catalogue geometry is training-only. A shuffled-response control and held-station/held-response evaluation are essential. The central ablation is observation-dependent uncertainty versus the same mixture with fixed uncertainty, because M-PLOC already anticipates measurement-dependent variance. No tail-bin multiplication is involved, but there is also no mathematical guarantee of lower tail error or unchanged bulk.

**Recommendation:** preserve the failed polarization gate and do not spend GPU budget on this branch. Continue the already separate physical-response-versus-station-prior experiment on its own preregistered evidence. A future method claim would need a specifically different implemented construction and matched1/3/5 magnitude-distribution gains; none has been established here.
