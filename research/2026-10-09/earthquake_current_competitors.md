# Additional EEW competitors and a matched implementation shortlist

Research checked 9 October 2026. This supplements `work/repo/research/2026-10-09/literature_review.md`; it is a targeted primary-source audit, not an exhaustive systematic review. No TEST waveform values were read, no model weights were executed, and no AWS jobs were started for this memo. Published numbers below are contextual and are **not a leaderboard against our Chile/INSTANCE results**.

The broad review missed three consequential comparisons: **FME (2026), DHLnet (2024), and E3WS (2023)**. FME is especially relevant because its authors already test higher-magnitude weighting with similar aggregate error. The foundation-model papers provide candidate encoders but do not establish superior accuracy at our 1/3/5-second deadlines. A Bayesian EEW paper initially identified as a possible competitor actually predicts PGA, so its scores must not be included in a magnitude table.

## Released-code audit: FME needs an explicit porting protocol

Audited source commit: `04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767` of [whywhy21/FME](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/tree/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767). Exact source URLs, hashes and sizes are saved in `competitor_sources/source_manifest.json`.

1. **Trigger clock and station gating.** `P02_create_dataset.py` sets `firs_ppick=4`, `Buffer_time=50`, `sw_step=50`, and `sw_length=2000`, at 100 Hz. It takes the minimum of E/N/Z P indices, removes nearby duplicate instruments, sorts by P arrival and retains ten. Thus this example selects **first arrivals**, while the article describes nearest stations. A station waveform is zero until the segment endpoint is at least 50 samples after its pick. Distance is nonzero only when the pick lies in the retained window and that trigger-delay condition is met. [Exporter](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/temp_01_data_process/P02_create_dataset.py)
2. **Future picks are metadata, not demonstrated streaming decisions.** The exporter reads fixed pick indices. The paper identifies RED-PAN as the picker, but the inspected pipeline does not rerun it using only the prefix. Selecting the first ten arrivals from a full list does not by itself expose future waveform samples: before ten arrivals, future slots are zero and an equivalent online slot-filling procedure is possible. Nevertheless, event eligibility (at least ten eventual stations), pick production latency, and changing availability require an explicit replay audit. The current code is insufficient evidence that every station mask is operationally available at the stated deadline. This is our assessment of the exporter, not a claim that the paper used future catalog picks.
3. **Example split discrepancy, not an accusation about the experiment.** P01 writes one row per `(eventID, time-slice)`; P02 applies `torch.utils.data.random_split` to the row-indexed Dataset. Under its documented all-slices CSV, that can place slices of one event in different subsets. The README calls this a **fine-tuning example**; its committed metadata contains only two events/136 station rows, and no split column. No separate partition generator/config is present in the inspected tree. This does **not prove** the published event-stratified experiments used this script. Do not run it unchanged as a fair baseline. [CSV generator](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/temp_02_model_pretrain/P01_dataset_csv.py), [training example](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/temp_02_model_pretrain/P02_train_model.py), [README](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/README.md).
4. **Prefix preprocessing matters.** The example converts raw full records and calls `scipy.signal.detrend(array)` before window slicing. Computing a full-record fitted trend may let later samples change early values. A strictly causal adaptation must estimate preprocessing from available samples and test future-suffix invariance. The taper also uses a pick index; apply it only once that pick is available. [Raw conversion](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/temp_01_data_process/P01_by_Factor.py), [processing utilities](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/FME_tools/FME_tools.py).
5. **Oracle versus operational distance.** The example directly reads `hypo_dist` from metadata containing catalog event coordinates. This is additional source information relative to our waveform+station-coordinate TEAM baseline. Keep a catalog-distance experiment clearly labelled **oracle**, and share exactly the same distance input with the baseline. A deployable comparison must instead use a frozen location model's prefix-only estimate; fitting that model requires TRAIN-only or out-of-fold predictions for training the magnitude head. Do not treat station coordinates as equivalent to source-to-station distance.

The repository includes a ~3.48 MB `FME_05Hz_model.pth.tar` file. We inspected its listing, not its payload. No LICENSE file was present in the audited tree; reimplement architecture from the description rather than importing code without resolving its terms. The example explicitly uses `gaussian=False`/MSE; an unused Gaussian-label option in its loader is not evidence that the published method outputs calibrated magnitude densities. [Loader](https://github.com/whywhy21/FME/blob/04d1c30cb0c7ea319b37c1ad63e00fa949063767/FME_tools/Loaddata.py)

Our inference: a foundation encoder may improve representation quality, but per-trace amplitude normalization can remove a major magnitude cue. A transferred encoder should receive prefix-only normalized waveforms **plus separately preserved physical log-amplitude/energy features**. This combination is established feature fusion, not a novelty claim. Frozen-versus-finetuned comparisons would determine whether transfer helps the tails rather than just the common magnitudes.

## Physical and probabilistic papers that affect the claim

**Li, 2022, generic rupture model.** A variational autoencoder organizes global source-time functions, including slowly developing runaway ruptures and complex shallow events; it cautions against extrapolating a universal magnitude-deterministic onset. SCARDEC and a megathrust STF collection are linked, but this is not a ready 1/3/5-second waveform-to-magnitude benchmark. It motivates a distribution over rupture duration/growth families instead of a single deterministic extrapolation. Any conversion from retrospective STFs to noisy local early waveforms is a new modeling assumption requiring validation. [Primary paper and data statement](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021GL096464)

**Liu et al., 2025, SVGD-BNN with elastic weight consolidation.** This is relevant distributional EEW prior art, but its target is **PGA**. Inputs include seven P-wave features; Bayesian parameter inference supplies uncertainty, and continual learning supplies adaptation. Its 3 s correlation/STD improvement and 3/5 s MSE values are not magnitude errors. No released executable weights were established. [Primary paper, §2.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/15/15/2718)

**WaveCastNet, 2025.** Sequence-to-sequence forecasting predicts wavefields without first estimating magnitude. It evaluates finite-fault extrapolation and uses bootstrap ensembles for uncertainty. Its example 5.7 s input and wavefield errors answer a different question from our magnitude MAE. The code is public, but it is a substantial new simulation/data task rather than a drop-in magnitude backbone. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65435-2), [code](https://github.com/dwlyu/WaveCastNet)

**Remaining source gaps.** Wang et al. 2022, *Weak Predictability of Rupture Growth Evidenced by P Waves*, DOI10.1785/0120210316, was discovered, but its primary publisher full text was inaccessible during this pass; no numerical result from it is used. The 2025 laboratory study *Under what circumstances is the final size of a laboratory earthquake predictable at the onset of the P-wave?* also needs full primary-text verification before detailed transfer claims. Laboratory acoustic ruptures do not establish field 1-second predictability. [Publisher landing page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012821X25002353)

No newly verified 2022–2026 paper in this pass supplies a released, cleanly matched Chile/INSTANCE 1/3/5 distribution benchmark that removes the need to reproduce TEAM-LM. This is a scoped search result, not proof that no such paper exists. Existing amplitude-shift density methods and the 2026 INSTANCE density paper remain important close prior art from the earlier review.

## Implement these two comparisons next

**1. E3WS-inspired causal feature baseline.** Extract time, band-energy/period, cepstral and polarization summaries from exactly the same available prefixes. Retain physical log-amplitude and causal integral features. Fit an event-weighted XGBoost point regressor first; use TRAIN-only grouped folds for any stacking. For Chile, aggregate station summaries under the same availability convention as TEAM; for INSTANCE, use the same single station. Report this as an adaptation because the preamble, network clock and station aggregation differ from E3WS. A modest feature baseline is a necessary challenge to a more complicated distribution correction. It could identify whether missing physical information, rather than uncertainty estimation, explains the tail error.

**2. FME-inspired spectral encoder with controlled geometry.** Reimplement a small Fourier+temporal encoder and train under our immutable event split and strict prefix cutoff. Start with the **same waveform and station-coordinate inputs** as TEAM; changing coordinate fusion from FME's catalog-distance correction is an explicit adaptation. Use both scalar and the same distribution head/loss as our method to separate encoder effects from objective effects. Do not wait for a fourth trigger or provide future catalog distance in the main comparison. A separate distance-oracle arm is useful only if both competing methods receive it. Chile has 8–21 stations per event, so retain all events with masks instead of silently requiring ten. Test higher-magnitude weighting as its own baseline, before adding a new auxiliary objective.

If only one additional neural run is affordable, a **SeisT-S DiTing transfer** is a feasible substitute for the second comparison: the public checkpoint is compact and its original region differs from Chile/INSTANCE. This remains lower priority than a well-trained TEAM-LM control and the feature baseline, because its early-prefix performance has not been established.

Selection should use the existing frozen calibration scheme, with all 3 deadlines evaluated jointly. For each candidate report natural-event MAE/RMSE and median absolute error, magnitude-conditioned errors with unique event counts, upper error quantiles/CVaR, signed underestimation, and proper probability scores. Calibrate any median/mean decision shift separately from the learned density. Keep TEST sealed until the comparison and selection rule are fixed. The scientific contribution must survive stronger physical/architecture controls; neither weighting large events, using a density, adding a prior, nor using a future-aware auxiliary target is new by itself.

## Additional scope check, 9 October 2026

**EarthESND (2026).** A multiscale echo-state reservoir with dendritic readout and conditional tabular augmentation addresses magnitude imbalance using 36,196 three-component records and 2–6 s windows. It is a useful low-compute comparator at 3/5 s; the accessible publisher text does not establish a matched 1 s result or our event/station split. Full split and augmentation details must be checked before numerical comparison. No published accuracy value is copied here from an unverified table. [Primary publisher page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0045790626002338), DOI10.1016/j.compeleceng.2026.111161.

**Generalized EEW networks (2024).** Zhang and Zhang combine detection, location and magnitude models, using recombined station observations, coordinate information and amplitude scaling for training. Their magnitude model retains normalization amplitude and an epicentral-distance input; its observation cutoff is varied during training. The integrated system and available station timing differ from our catalog-P single-station INSTANCE setting. Useful controls are horizontal rotation, retained physical amplitude and explicit location uncertainty; their paper is not evidence that a calibrated final-magnitude PDF is new. [Primary open-access paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11488472/), [author code](https://github.com/zxiong218/EEWNet).

**Sequential intensity PDFs (2026).** Kumano and colleagues construct forecast-dependent likelihoods and sequentially update seismic-intensity distributions from JMA EEW forecasts, with highest-density regions and three earthquake examples. This concerns intensity rather than direct magnitude inference, but further limits a broad novelty claim for sequential probability updating in EEW. Only the publisher abstract was verified; the full text is marked restricted. [Primary abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jscejj/82/13/82_25-13321/_article/-char/en), DOI10.2208/jscejj.25-13321.


## Additional rupture-growth and censoring prior art (9 October, 19:13 UTC)

Longobardi, Colombelli and Zollo (2025) report magnitude-dependent early displacement growth across more than 7,000 records. Their slope interval is defined using a fraction of a detected later plateau time, so adopting that exact interval in a fixed 1-second predictor would require an additional online estimation step. Our inference: fixed-prefix growth summaries are defensible features; treating a retrospectively determined plateau as already observed would invalidate the deadline. The paper explicitly identifies real-time plateau estimation and uncertainty as open implementation challenges. [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02814-z).

Zeng et al. (2025), STF-MgNet, combine a Transformer and U-Net to reconstruct a complete source-time function from an initial segment, with waveform and gradient losses. The source functions come from retrospective inversions; preprocessing resamples each complete function to 1,000 points, and models are trained for fractions of total duration. Our inference: this supplies prior art for future-rupture supervision, but its stated timing/accuracy is not directly comparable with a fixed wall-clock station P-wave experiment. A transferable control is future-shape or growth supervision using training targets only and strictly fixed observed prefixes. [Primary article, methods §§2.1–2.4](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2025JH000801).

Aiello et al. use censored survival and cure models for smartphone triggering, estimating epicentre, depth and origin time. This is existing EEW prior art for extracting information from an event that has not happened yet. It is not the same observation or target as magnitude updating from a running amplitude record, but a claim that censoring or Bayesian survival inference is new to EEW would be false. The pending innovation experiment tests a narrower amplitude likelihood construction against matched alternatives. [Primary journal article](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssa/article/189/1/314/7953229).
