# Two distribution experiments — frozen before fitting

The response-conditioning and exposure-correction experiments are ready for controlled AWS testing at 1, 3 and 5 seconds. **Neither has a real-data accuracy result yet.** Both implementations passed isolated Codex review. The reviewed shared export passed 33 focused tests on AWS; the promoted response runner passed 11 synthetic tests and the exposure runner passed 39. The complete waveform export finished in 302.35 seconds; its array and completion hashes passed parent verification before fitting. A separate metadata audit passed all 112,660 identities, weights, validity masks and response descriptors, scanning every 979,487 saved join row; it did not reopen waveform contents. Job-specific launchers also passed review. All runs use the same 112,660 selected TRAIN records and the completed polarization study's event/station partitions, weights and deadline-specific validity masks.

| Experiment | Fixed comparisons | Mechanism being tested |
|---|---|---|
| Response conditioning | Counts/late metadata; native amplitude/late metadata; native amplitude/early conditioning; coherent digital-gain augmentation. Four arms, two seeds, ten epochs. | Whether conditioning the encoder on the nominal acquisition response before its final nonlinearity helps beyond giving the same metadata to an equally sized late-conditioning model. |
| Exposure correction | Weighted Huber plus CE; natural-population CE; corrected CE with extra exposure for sparse magnitude bands. Three arms, two seeds, ten epochs. | Whether more rare-magnitude training observations help while correcting the induced training prior and retaining a natural-population probability distribution at inference. |

Response C versus B has identical parameter counts, initial outputs, training order and loss. Its primary decision requires high-magnitude improvement in both seeds at every deadline and in both seen-station and held-station event panels, with bulk MAE/MedAE changes no greater than 0.002. Paired event bootstrap intervals assess precision. Full-response shape attribution would still require a later scalar-only ablation and external transfer test.

The exposure correction is an established imbalance-training control. Sampling uses `Q_i ∝ w_i a_y`, training uses `CE(z + log(a), y)`, and inference uses `softmax(z)`. Restoration weights enter once. Its own fixed criteria require an event-macro M≥4 improvement of at least 0.02 and small bulk changes in all six panels. An algebraically correct population correction does not guarantee finite-sample calibration.

The grids are separate experiments. Response uses support `0.0, 0.1, …, 6.5`; exposure uses `0.05, 0.15, …, 6.55`, as independently frozen in their protocols. The same PMF therefore produces a point decision 0.05 larger under exposure, and its Huber gradient differs. Their sampling schedules also differ. **Do not pool their baselines or interpret cross-grid differences as an architecture/loss ablation.** This decision was made before either real training grid ran.

The response audit was corrected before export: unresolved digital clocks must not be replaced by ObsPy's arbitrary 1 Hz fallback. Eighteen response descriptions changed, and 93 channel epochs now have unavailable response descriptors. The 8,164 epoch identities and all sampled record identities/weights are unchanged. Complete-response coverage among metadata-eligible TRAIN records changes from 847,723 to 847,165 of 963,631; records remain included with explicit masks. Coverage differs by family: HH 99.80%, EH 66.58%, HN 81.36%. These are metadata coverage figures, not waveform validity rates.

The acquisition descriptors are nominal full-chain metadata, not an effective inverse of INSTANCE's full-record preprocessing. Scalar sensitivity division does not fully recover ground motion or reconcile velocity and acceleration spectra. Absolute gain remains in late metadata, so the complete model is not structurally gain invariant. The augmentation changes counts and sensitivity coherently; it is a digital representation test, not a simulation of larger earthquakes.

The held panels contain 66 M≥4 events each and only two M≥5 events. Earlier full-TRAIN project studies used these events, so this new partition is held out from the new fits, not a globally untouched external benchmark. Manual P picks, released preprocessing, station dependence and bounded categorical support remain limitations. A positive pilot would justify a specific next experiment; it would not establish novelty, M7–9 extrapolation or superiority to a published paper.

Execution uses the existing original A10G, fresh directories, exact source/data hashes, fixed budgets and job-specific stop controls. No new AWS instance or volume is required. The separate Chile baseline continues its fixed 100-epoch budget; the latest verified log contains 94 completed event epochs, with epoch 95 active. Its TEST waveforms remain unopened.
