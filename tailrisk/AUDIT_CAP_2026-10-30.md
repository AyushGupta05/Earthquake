# Adversarial audit: capped-training extrapolation (eqgr / eqdgr), 30 Oct 2026

Scope: the claims in FINDINGS.md 04:15, 04:25 and 04:30 UTC (30 Oct) and THEORY.md Proposition 3.
Everything was read-only. All computation ran on CPU with `nice`; the only files written are in
`/mnt/scratch/audit_cap/` on the main host and in this scratchpad. gpu2 runs read scripts from stdin
and printed to stdout.

## Verdict

**The core claim holds.** A capped equivariant head predicts held-out events above the cap far
better than an identically capped free mixture. No check found a leak or an error that makes this
result invalid:

- The cap filter is correct in every fold of every arm.
- No magnitude information reaches training through other inputs.
- Folds are sequence-pure.
- The headline numbers reproduce to three decimals from the saved npz files.
- The capped-vs-capped gap survives independent Chile magnitude labels and is unanimous across GK
  clusters (8 of 8 on Chile; 11 of 11 on INSTANCE in `robust_instance_cap40.md`).

Three secondary statements are overstated or confounded:

1. **Chile:** the claim that the capped head beats the free mixture trained on all magnitudes
   (.65 vs .86) holds only on MA labels.
2. **INSTANCE:** the headline arm uses the joint-truncation objective that the authors themselves
   flagged as leaky.
3. **Chile GR heads:** they train with a label-binning bug at the cap.

There is also no classical baseline. A three-parameter amplitude–distance law fitted below the cap
also extrapolates; on Chile at 3 s it matches the M≥6 headline when given catalogue distance.

## 1. Cap filter — severity: none

**What I did.**
- Read `train.py` (`main`, `train_fold`).
- Recomputed each fold's training set independently from `meta.npz` as
  `(fold != k) & (mag < cap − 1e-6)` (float32, as in training).
- Compared the counts with `record.json["train_records"]` for every capped arm on both hosts.

**What I found.**
- `tr` is filtered (`train.py` line 222) before `train_fold`. Inside `train_fold`, everything is
  derived from the capped `train_idx`: `fit_static_norm`, `label_weights` (`weighting='none'` for
  every arm, so the weights are ones), `steps_per_epoch` and the permutation.
- No `amp_aug` is used in the capped GR arms; `train.py` forbids it for them.
- Independent counts equal the `record.json` counts in every fold:
  - INSTANCE: 641,740 / 1,130,111 / 939,859 / 940,694 / 902,928, maximum label 3.9.
  - Chile: 441,901 / 378,860 / 441,890 / 378,833 / 378,832, maximum label 4.99.
- These counts are the same for every capped arm (`mix_cap40`, `eqgr_cap40`, `eqdgr_cap40`,
  `eqdgr_cap40_bfix`, `mix_cap50`, `eqgr_cap50`, `eqgr_cap50_bfix`, `mixgr_cap50`, `eqdgr_cap50`,
  `eqdgr_cap50_cond`).
- The filter removed 14k–19k records per fold on INSTANCE and about 450–490 on Chile.
- INSTANCE labels just below the cap are only 3.9, on a 0.1 grid. Chile has 3-decimal labels up to
  4.99, which matters for finding D.
- Code and data are identical on the two hosts: md5 of `train.py`, `models.py`, `common.py`,
  `train_std_full.npy`, `meta.npz` and `scale.npy` match.
- The uncapped arm copies on gpu2 are byte-identical to the main host.
- The code that trained the gpu2 arms (commits e8b4059 and c9290f4) has the same filter and the
  same `effective_log_prior`.

**What I did.**
- Checked that every cluster and every event is assigned to one fold.
- For each tail event, counted other-fold events inside its own GK window, and within 20 km and

## 4. Recomputation from the saved npz files — severity: none

**What I did.**
- Wrote `audit_eval.py`, which loads `rows` and `pmf` per fold and takes the posterior median from
  the PMF. The PMF is normalized to its own mass and treated as uniform within each ±.05 bin.
- Compared the result with the fine-grid median stored in `dec[..., 1]` (`wmed_r1`).

Median code:

```python
p = pmf.astype(np.float64); tot = p.sum(-1, keepdims=True)
F = np.cumsum(p, -1) / tot                      # CDF at upper bin edges
k = np.minimum((F < 0.5).sum(-1), p.shape[-1] - 1)
Fprev = np.where(k > 0, np.take_along_axis(F, np.maximum(k - 1, 0)[..., None], -1)[..., 0], 0.0)
pk = np.take_along_axis(p / tot, k[..., None], -1)[..., 0]
med = CENTERS[k] - 0.05 + 0.1 * np.clip((0.5 - Fprev) / np.maximum(pk, 1e-12), 0, 1)
```

## 5. Cap mask active in training — severity: none (D is a separate bug)

**What I did.**
- Instantiated `MagnitudeModel` on CPU for `eqgr`, `eqdgr` (joint and conditional) and `mixgr`
  with `train_cap=5.0`.
- Compared P(m ≥ cap) in `train()` and `eval()` mode.
- Read the training loop.

**What I found.**
- `train_cap` reaches the GR module: `head` for eqgr and mixgr, `head.prior` for eqdgr.
- In train mode P(m ≥ cap) = 0 exactly for all four heads. In eval mode it is nonzero (for example
  .00 / .07 / .65 / .95 for A = −6 … −3).
- `model.train()` is called at the start of every epoch and nothing switches to eval before the
  loss. `self.training` propagates to `head.prior`.
- Prediction runs after `model.eval()`, so the factor is untruncated at test time, as intended.

**Separate bug (D).** `Binned.log_prob_label` rounds the label to the 0.1 bin:
`round((y + 1.0) * 10)`. A Chile label in [4.95, 5.00) therefore maps to the 5.0 bin, which
`effective_log_prior` masks because `centers >= cap − 1e-6`.

- I verified the mapping directly: labels 4.95, 4.96 and 4.99 all map to the masked 5.0 bin.
- 84 / 69 / 70 / 41 / 72 training records per fold are affected. Each contributes about 1e4 nats
  that can never be reduced.
- Signature 1, loss spikes: `fold0 ep3/ep7 step0` losses of 11.9 and 11.7 in `eqgr_cap50.log` and
  `mixgr_cap50.log`, and 13.0 in `eqdgr_cap50*.log`. That is 1e4/1024 ≈ 9.8 above baseline, i.e.
  one such record in the batch. There are no spikes in `eqgr_nll.log` or `mix_cap50.log`.
- Signature 2, learned slope: the gradient raises the tail slope β, which never reaches an
  equilibrium. Learned β per fold:

  | arm | learned β per fold |
  |---|---|
  | `eqgr_cap50` | −.30, −.20, −.30, −.21, −.24 |
  | `eqgr_nll` | −.66, −.54, −.67, −.53, −.62 |
  | `mixgr_cap50` | −.004, .018, .037, .009, .036 |
  | `eqgr_cap40` (INSTANCE, no affected labels) | −.82 … −1.93 |
  | `eqgr_nll` (INSTANCE) | −.47 … −1.20 |

  On INSTANCE, capping makes β *steeper*; on Chile it makes β flatter. That reversal is consistent
  with the bug acting on Chile.
- Impact on the headline is small:
  - `eqgr_cap50_bfix` (β pinned) gives the same tail (.650 / .540).
  - Near-cap bias of `eqgr_cap50` is only .01–.07 less negative than `eqgr_nll`.
  - Per-event M≥6 predictions of `eqgr_cap50` and `eqgr_nll` differ by a mean of .009 / −.015
    (mean absolute .10 / .09).
- Impact on the controls: the 04:15 and 04:30 entries attribute `mixgr_cap50`'s behaviour to
  Proposition 3. That behaviour is β ≈ 0, a bias of +.95 / +1.18 / +1.25 at M4.0–4.5, FP of
  149 per 10⁴ on small records at 3 s, and a negative amplitude response. The bug adds an active
  incentive to put likelihood mass in the masked bin, so that attribution is confounded.
- Fix: filter or mask on the *binned* label (cap at bin 4.95), or mask only bins whose lower edge
  is ≥ cap. Then rerun the Chile GR arms.

## 6. Label circularity — severity: material (A)

**What I did.**
- Scored the existing Chile predictions, trained on MA, against the independent `M_EXT` and
  `MW_SIPPL` columns (`/mnt/scratch/chile_relabel_*/meta.npz`, `label_ok` rows only).
- Split INSTANCE M≥5 events by magnitude type.

**What I found, Chile.**
- MA runs .1–.4 above M_EXT for the large events. For example, Tocopilla 2007 is MA 8.05 and
  M_EXT 7.72; MA 6.18 / 6.11 events are M_EXT 5.78 / 5.75.
- **Capped vs capped survives:**

  | label set | `eqgr_cap50` | `mix_cap50` |
  |---|---|---|
  | M_EXT M≥6 (9 events, 7 clusters) | .692 / .573 | 1.468 / 1.443 |
  | MW_SIPPL M≥6 (6 events, 5 clusters) | .673 / .614 | 1.405 / 1.381 |

- **The comparison with the full-data mixture does not survive at 3 s:**

  | label set | `eqgr_cap50` | `mix_nll` (seeds 1 / 2 / 3) |
  |---|---|---|
  | M_EXT, 3 s | .692 | .703 / .673 / .691 |
  | M_EXT, 5 s | .573 | .708 / .687 / .706 |
  | MW_SIPPL, 3 s | .673 | .687 / .616 / .647 |
  | MW_SIPPL, 5 s | .614 | .673 / .659 / .663 |

  By event count `eqgr_cap50` is still closer on 6–7 of 9 M_EXT events (5 of 7 clusters), so the
  data are underpowered either way.
- The ".65 vs .86 at 3 s" sentence ("beats the free mixture trained with them") should be stated
  as MA-specific.

**What I found, INSTANCE.**
- The Mw check has two weaknesses as an answer to circularity:
  - The 22 Mw events fall in 7 sequences, 9 of them in Central Italy.
  - In Italy, ML and Mw are close over M5–6.5, so Mw labels do not separate a log-amplitude label
    from a physical size in this range.
- The split runs opposite to what circularity predicts. On the 9 ML-labelled M≥5 events
  (event-level MAE at 3 s, from the per-event table), `eqdgr_cap40` .684, `eqgr_cap40` .751 and
  `mix_nll` .667 are about equal. On Mw events `eqdgr_cap40` .533 beats `mix_nll` .722.
- So the INSTANCE advantage tracks event size (the Mw events are the larger ones), not label type.
  The Chile relabelling is the stronger test, and it gives the result above.

## 7. Everything else

**B — the INSTANCE headline arm uses the leaky objective (material).**
- `eqdgr_cap40` trained from 00:41 to 02:44 UTC. Its `record.json` has no `cap_latent`, and
  `cap_conditional` arrived at 04:18 (commit 29dbe5c), so it used joint truncation.
- The leak's gross form (bulk MAE ≈ 4, as `eqdgr_cap50` shows on Chile) did not occur. A milder
  form did: the share of M<3 records with P(m≥5) > .3 is 2.0 / 2.0 / 2.0 per 10⁴ for
  `eqdgr_cap40`, against .3 / .1 / .1 for `eqdgr_nll`.
- Reporting `eqgr_cap40` (1.097 / .671 / .554) or the pending `eqdgr_cap40_cond` would be
  consistent with the Chile choice. The conclusion is unchanged: `mix_cap40` is at 1.81 / 1.61 / 1.57.

**C — no classical baseline (material for framing).**
- `linear_baseline.py` computes A and the 8 invariants exactly as the networks see them
  (`common.amplitude_features`) and fits each fold on the capped training rows only.

| baseline | Chile M≥6 MAE (1 / 3 / 5 s) | INSTANCE M≥5 MAE (1 / 3 / 5 s) | bulk MAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward OLS M ~ A (+ invariants, + log R) | ≥ 2.1, heavily attenuated | ≥ 2.1, heavily attenuated | — |
| Inverse law A = a + bM + c log R, catalogue distance (oracle) | 1.21 / .61 / .59 | 1.01 / .80 / .71 | .34 / .97 |
| Same law without distance (invariants as covariates) | biased +.6 … +1.6 | biased +2.1 … +3.6 | — |

- With the oracle distance, the inverse law matches `eqgr_cap50` on Chile at 1–3 s, but is worse
  on INSTANCE and far worse on bulk.
- Selection on the regressor M does not bias the inverse regression, so it is a valid
  capped-training baseline.
- The neural head's real advantage is extrapolating *without* a distance input and with good bulk
  accuracy. A reviewer will ask for this comparison, and the free mixture is a weak comparator for
  extrapolation as such.

**E — hidden mixture components (minor).**
- `hidden_components.py` rebuilds the fold-0 models on CPU, runs them on 3,000 held-out records with
  M < cap − 2 plus all M ≥ cap records, and measures the likelihood weight on components centred
  above cap + .5.
- Weight on those components for the small records, 1 / 3 / 5 s:

  | arm | mean weight | records with weight > .05 |
  |---|---|---|
  | `eqgr_cap40` (INSTANCE) | .0016 / .0010 / .0007 | 0.43 / 0.27 / 0.30 % |
  | `eqgr_nll` (INSTANCE) | .0002 / .0002 / .0001 | 0.13 / 0.10 / 0.03 % |
  | `eqgr_cap50` (Chile) | 0 | 0 |

- Mean P(m≥5 | M<3) is .0011 / .0009 / .0008 for `eqgr_cap40` against .0002 / .0001 / .0001 for
  `eqgr_nll`.
- Proposition 3's remark should cover mixtures: the component index is a latent, so hidden
  components are possible.

**F — the amplitude-response figure is tautological (minor).** `cap_response.py` line 66 computes
`model.trunk(shape, A + delta, …)` and `model.head(h, A + delta)`. For the equivariant heads, `h`
does not depend on A, and in the linear GR region the posterior shifts by exactly κ(s)·δ. The
statement "κ learned below the cap equals κ learned on all data, to within .04" is informative.
"Keeps rising linearly" is guaranteed by the architecture. Amplified small-event waveforms also keep
small-event shapes, so the test cannot probe real large-event waveforms.

**G — small and clustered samples (minor).** Capped vs capped is unanimous across clusters
(Chile 8 of 8 at 3 and 5 s; INSTANCE 11 of 11). Capped `eqgr` vs full-data `mix_nll` on Chile MA
is 7 of 8 clusters. The capped arms are single-seed; the uncapped eqgr seed spread on Chile M≥6 is
.601 / .612 / .608 at 3 s.

**Median action and evaluation subset (none).**
- The evaluation rows are identical across all compared arms, and every fold is fully scored.
- The mean action gives the same ordering (fold summaries in `record.json`; mean-action table in
  `robust_instance_cap40.md`).
- No events are selected out of the test set: all 12 M≥6 Chile train/dev events (manifest) and all
  31 INSTANCE M≥5 events are present.
- I checked whether Chile picks are late for large events. They are not: the median residual from P
  moveout is −.93 s for M≥6 and −.70 s for M<3, and 0 of 71 M≥6 picks are S-like.

**Physical sanity (note, not a flaw).** `eqgr_cap50` predicts 7.17 at 3 s for Tocopilla (MA 8.05,
M_EXT 7.72). That is above the 3 s "physical limit ≈ M6.5–6.7" quoted in the 04:15 entry. The pick
is an ordinary P pick, so the quoted limit, not the data, looks too strict. Rupture directivity
compresses more than 3 s of rupture into a 3 s window.

## Recommended fixes

1. Rebin before capping on Chile (mask on the bin's lower edge, or filter on the binned label <
   cap), then rerun `eqgr_cap50`, `mixgr_cap50` and `eqdgr_cap50_cond`.
2. Report `eqgr_cap40` or `eqdgr_cap40_cond` as the INSTANCE headline, not the joint-truncated
   `eqdgr_cap40`.
3. State the "beats the full-data mixture" result for Chile as MA-only, and add the M_EXT and
   MW_SIPPL rows.
4. Add a classical capped baseline: an inverse amplitude–distance law, with oracle distance and
   with an estimated one.
5. Extend Proposition 3's remark to the mixture component index, and report P(m ≥ cap + 1) false
   alarms, not just FP4.
6. Present the amplitude response as a κ comparison, not as evidence of extrapolation.
