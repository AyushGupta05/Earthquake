# Adversarial audit of the eqgr / eqdgr evidence — 30 Oct 2026

Scope: does anything invalidate or inflate the claim that the proposed heads (`eqgr`, `eqdgr`)
beat the baselines, `mix_nll` above all? Code audited at commit `add200d` (HEAD on the host; the
uncommitted `train_cap` / `magnet_` edits in progress at the time of the audit do not touch any
existing arm). Claims audited: `README.md` and the newest `FINDINGS.md` entries (19–30 Oct).
All checks were read-only. Audit scripts and their raw outputs are on the host in
`/mnt/scratch/audit/` (`audit_*.py`, `*_out.json`).

**Bottom line.** I found no leakage that invalidates the comparison. Folds, held stations, input
causality, training settings and the evaluation arithmetic all check out, and an independent
recomputation from the saved fold PMFs reproduces the reported numbers to four decimals. The
M≥4 claims are robust. Four problems inflate the headline:

1. The M≥5 gains come mostly from one aftershock sequence (Central Italy, about 72% of every M≥5
   gain). With only 11 sequences behind them, their "CI excludes zero" status does not survive a
   small-cluster correction.
2. The false-M4 rates in five places are 10× too small (per 10³ labelled per 10⁴). This hides
   that `eqdgr` roughly **doubles** the false-alarm rate on unseen stations.
3. The Chile tail gain mostly disappears when the same predictions are scored against the
   catalogue's own independent magnitudes. That gain is largely agreement with the MA label,
   which is itself derived from amplitudes.
4. The significance statements rest on treating dependent units as independent (the 45-cell
   sign tests) and on selecting the model on the test folds (eqdgr, "single best model", best
   fusion rule).

Severity scale: **Critical** = invalidates a headline claim; **High** = materially inflates a
headline number or its significance; **Medium** = qualifies a claim or a secondary result;
**Low** = hygiene or small, arm-neutral effects.

## Findings

### H1 (High). The M≥5 evidence is 11 sequences, and one of them carries about 72% of every M≥5 gain

**Evidence.** The 31 M≥5 events fall in only **11 GK clusters**. Three of them hold 61% of the
1,757 M≥5 records: Central Italy (cluster 0, fold 0) 28%, Emilia (cluster 3, fold 1) 19%,
L'Aquila (cluster 1, fold 2) 14% (`meta_out.json`). The table below is my recomputation from
`arms/*/fold*.npz` (`head_out.json`). Δ is the record-level M≥5 MAE at 5 s, arm − mix (median
action). LOCO = leave one cluster out.

| comparison | Δ (point) | share of Δ from Central Italy | Δ without Central Italy | LOCO range | bootstrap 95% (evaluate.py scheme) | delete-one-cluster jackknife 95% (t, 10 df) | clusters with Δ<0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eqgr − mix, seed 1 | −.155 | 74% | −.057 | [−.193, −.057] | [−.288, −.023] | [−.380, +.071] | 8/11 |
| eqdgr − mix, seed 1 | −.200 | 72% | −.077 | [−.252, −.077] | [−.371, −.022] | [−.488, +.089] | 7/11 |
| eqgr − mix, 3-seed ensembles | −.162 | 70% | −.068 | [−.195, −.068] | [−.291, −.043] | [−.377, +.053] | 10/11 |
| eqdgr − mix, 3-seed ensembles | −.206 | 70% | −.086 | [−.258, −.086] | [−.375, −.036] | [−.490, +.077] | 8/11 (+1 zero) |
| eqdgr_hs − mix_hs, held stations | −.233 | 64% | −.122 | [−.261, −.122] | [−.369, −.072] | [−.482, +.016] | 9/11 |
| eqgr_hs − mix_hs, held stations | −.147 | 56% | −.094 | [−.154, −.094] | [−.209, −.067] | **[−.264, −.030]** | 10/11 |

Per-fold Δ at 5 s (seed 1): eqgr −.40 / +.005 / −.10 / −.10 / −.02 and eqdgr −.51 / +.017 /
−.07 / −.19 / −.007 for folds 0–4. Fold 0 alone is the 2016–17 sequence.

**Impact.** The sign of the M≥5 gain is real and fairly consistent: every LOCO Δ is negative,
24–27 of 31 events improve, and eqgr's ensemble wins 10 of 11 sequences (two-sided sign test
p ≈ .012). Its size is not. Outside Central Italy the M≥5 gain is −.06 to −.09, not −.15 to
−.21. The 95% intervals quoted as "excluding zero" (README §Result tables, the M≥5 row of
§Three catalogues, the held-station paragraph) come from a percentile bootstrap. That bootstrap
draws from 19,460 clusters, but only 11 carry the M≥5 metric, which makes it anti-conservative.
A small-G-robust interval includes zero for every M≥5 comparison except eqgr on held stations.
"31 M≥5 events" overstates the effective sample: it is closer to 11 units, dominated by one.
The **M≥4 claims are robust**: 194 clusters; the eqgr ensemble's M≥4 Δ at 5 s is −.028, with
jackknife CI [−.055, −.001], LOCO range [−.030, −.014] and 231/346 events better.

**Fix.** Report M≥5 as "n = 31 events in 11 sequences", with:
- the Δ without Central Italy next to the pooled Δ;
- cluster-level sign counts;
- a small-cluster interval (cluster jackknife with t(G−1), or a wild-cluster bootstrap) instead
  of the plain percentile bootstrap.

Drop "CI excludes zero" for M≥5 unless it survives that interval. Event-level M≥5 deltas
(eqgr ensemble −.141, eqdgr ensemble −.175) should sit beside the record-level ones.

The 19–20 Oct entries (README.md:37, 52, 54, 124; FINDINGS.md:664, 921, 944–966) use the right

**Impact.** README's "What to deploy" recommends `eqdgr` for single stations, and its
held-station paragraph presents the false-alarm cost as negligible ("+.4 to +.9 per 10⁴"). In
fact the false-M4 rate on unseen stations doubles at 1 s (17.9 vs 9.0 per 10⁴, CI excludes
zero) and rises 1.6–2× at 3–5 s. On seen stations it also doubles at 1 s. The 20-epoch
"excess disappears" is a reduction from +4.8 to +1.6 per 10⁴ with an interval reaching +2.9.
For an EEW system, false alerts are a first-order cost.

**Fix.** Correct the five passages. Add the absolute false-alarm rates to every eqdgr
recommendation, and state the held-station doubling plainly.

### M1 (Medium–High). The Chile tail gain largely disappears when scored against Chile's independent magnitude columns

**Evidence.** Chile labels are `MA` (`chile_prep.py:133`). MA is continuous: 500,464 of 505,666
labels are off the 0.1 grid. It is the catalogue's amplitude-derived magnitude, correlation
.97 with ML. On MA ≥ 5 events it sits +.11 above MW_SIPPL (`dups_out.json`). The source table
also carries MW_SIPPL, M_EXT and ML. I scored the existing three-seed ensemble predictions
against those columns, selecting the tail by the same column, so no retraining was involved
(`chile2_out.json`). Δ is eqdgr ensemble − mix ensemble, MAE, 1 / 3 / 5 s:

| tail selected and scored by | events (clusters) | Δ vs that label | Δ vs MA on the same records |
|---|---|---|---|
| MA ≥ 6 (as reported) | 12 (8) | — | **−.01 / −.27 / −.38** |
| M_EXT ≥ 6 | 9 (7) | +.065 / −.075 / −.180 | +.04 / −.26 / −.38 |
| MW_SIPPL ≥ 6 | 6 (5) | +.097 / −.057 / −.085 | +.07 / −.30 / −.37 |
| ML ≥ 6 | 11 (8) | +.023 / −.091 / −.141 | −.02 / −.30 / −.40 |
| M_EXT ≥ 5 | 65 (29) | +.028 / +.016 / −.022 | +.01 / −.04 / −.08 |
| MW_SIPPL ≥ 5 | 44 (21) | +.036 / +.014 / −.016 | +.01 / −.05 / −.09 |

eqgr behaves the same way: M_EXT ≥ 6 gives +.155 / −.060 / −.198. Measured with MA the Chile
M≥6 gain is robust: all 8 clusters negative, LOCO −.33 to −.42 at 5 s.

**Impact.** About half to three quarters of the M≥6 gain at 3–5 s, and nearly all of the M≥5
gain, exists only relative to MA. Against independent magnitudes the equivariant heads are worse
at 1 s, and the 3 s gain is small. The models were trained on MA, so part of the shrinkage is a
scale mismatch. Still, the README's "Three catalogues, one model" row for Chile (−.28 / −.39)
and its "M≥6 error .48 vs .86" describe fidelity to an amplitude-based label. An equivariant
amplitude head is the model class most likely to reproduce such a label. It does not show
better recovery of physical size.

**Fix.** Retrain the Chile mix and eqdgr arms on M_EXT or MW_SIPPL; one seed each is about 2 h.
Until then, report the alternative-label scores next to the MA ones and describe MA's
provenance. GEOFON's top-tail labels raise a similar but milder concern: M≥7 records are
`Mw(mB)` 331 and `Mw(Mwp)` 146, both computed from P-wave amplitudes. GEOFON is reported as
mixed anyway.

### M2 (Medium). The 45-cell sign tests treat dependent cells as independent

**Evidence.** `seed_consistency.py:37–40, 70–86` runs a binomial test over 3 seeds × 5 folds ×
3 deadlines = 45 cells. Within a fold, the three seeds and three deadlines share the same test
events, so the cells are strongly correlated. The 9 eqgr exceptions all sit in one fold (Emilia),
and so do 7 of eqdgr's 12 M≥5 losses.

**Impact.** The quoted p-values are not valid: README.md:57–58 "33/45 … p = .002";
FINDINGS.md:238–257; 19 Oct 18:30 "36/45, p = 7×10⁻⁵". With 5 folds the smallest attainable
two-sided fold-level sign-test p is .0625. Cluster-level sign tests for M≥5 at 5 s, from H1:
- eqgr ensemble: 10/11, p ≈ .012
- eqdgr ensemble: 8/10 nonzero, p ≈ .11
- eqdgr seed 1: 7/11, p ≈ .55

**Fix.** Use the sequence (or fold) as the unit. Report seed agreement descriptively.

### M3 (Medium). The model is selected on the same CV folds that report its performance

**Evidence.** `eqgr` was pre-specified. I verified this: `EquivariantGRHead` and
`--arm eqgr_nll` are in commit `16bffd1` (18 Oct 23:23 UTC), before eq_nll's results (19 Oct
02:05 UTC, `logs/queue.log`).

Everything else was chosen after looking at the same five folds:
- `eqdgr` was "designed after seeing the eqgr results" (FINDINGS.md:1031–1043).
- About 25 head, width, epoch, slope and seed configurations were evaluated there.
- The 20-epoch eqdgr was then named "the single best model of the study" (README.md:151;
  FINDINGS.md:204).
- Fusion results are quoted "with the best rule per head", chosen on test results
  (README.md:222–224; FINDINGS.md:57).

README.md:257 says "nothing on a held-out fold is selected". That is true within an arm, not
across arms.

**Impact.** Winner's-curse optimism attaches to every eqdgr and 20-epoch INSTANCE number and to
the fused "best rule" comparisons. Out-of-sample support for eqdgr comes from Chile, which was
built after eqdgr was designed, but see M1. GEOFON supports it only at M≥7, at a bulk cost.

**Fix.** Lock eqdgr (10 epochs) and evaluate it once on data not used for selection. Options are
a new catalogue, a later INSTANCE period, or at least a single pre-registered fused rule.
Rephrase "single best model" as a post-hoc observation.

### M4 (Medium). Multi-station fusion counts a station's co-located sensors as separate stations

**Evidence.**
- 298,122 event–station pairs have more than one record (HH+HN, EH+HN). Together they hold
  596,244 records, 51% of all records (`meta_out.json`).
- `multistation.py:184–196` and `fusion_timeline.py:49–56` order records by catalogue
  hypocentral distance and never deduplicate by station. Co-located sensors share `hyp_km`, so
  they come out adjacent.
- The "k = 2 nearest stations" are the same station twice in 33% of all events, 35% of M≥4
  events and 39% of M≥5 events.
- Events have 1.24–1.30 records per unique station (`fd_out.json`): "median ≈ 15 stations" is
  12 unique stations, and 50 vs 45 for M≥5 events.

**Impact.** The naive product multiplies two near-identical observations, double-counting both
the likelihood and the prior. This exaggerates how much the naive product fails and how sharp
every fused posterior is. The k-curves and fused MAEs in the 21–22 Oct fusion entries and in
README §Multi-station fusion / §Event magnitude vs time are biased. The qualitative conclusion
(divide out the training prior) probably stands, since the over-counting it describes is real
even at one instrument per station. The single-station claims are unaffected.

**Fix.** Keep one instrument per station and event, or average the log-posteriors of one
station's instruments first. Then rerun `multistation.py` and `fusion_timeline.py` with k
counting stations.

### L1 (Low). Reported Δ values are bootstrap means, not observed differences

**Evidence.** `evaluate.py:141` (and `geofon_eval.py:69`, `multistation.py:158`) report
`d.mean()` of the bootstrap distribution as the Δ. For sparse tails the two differ:

| comparison | reported (bootstrap mean) | observed |
|---|---|---|
| eqgr − mix, M≥5 at 5 s | −.141 | −.155 |
| ensembles, same | −.149 | −.162 |
| eqdgr_hs held, M≥5 at 3 / 5 s | −.201 / −.217 | −.217 / −.233 |
| held-station FP4, eqdgr_hs − mix_hs at 1 s | −3.5 per 10⁴ | −8.5 per 10⁴ |

**Impact.** Mostly conservative for MAE, because the bootstrap pulls the Central Italy-driven
M≥5 Δ toward zero. It can mislead for rates. **Fix.** Report the point estimate with the
percentile interval.

### L2 (Low). Held stations: exclusion verified; small co-location residue

**Passed.**
- The rng(0) 20% mask (123 of 631 stations, 279,812 records) matches the saved `held_station`
  arrays in all 10 `*_hs` arms.
- Every fold's training count equals |fold≠f ∧ ¬held|.
- Static normalization is fit after the held rows are removed (`train.py:57, 209–210`;
  `common.py:82–85`).

**Residue.**
- 9 held stations have a training station within 1 km, 28 within 5 km. About 2.2k held records
  (0.8%) sit at a site with a co-located seen station.
- 4 held station codes reappear under another network in training (1,058 records).
- `common.fallback_gains` takes family medians over all rows, including held stations. This is
  instrument metadata, not labels.

**Impact.** Negligible. **Fix (optional).** Hold out by site (cluster stations within 1 km)
rather than by `NET.STA`.

### L3 (Low). GK mainshock windows without chaining leave peripheral aftershocks of tail events in other folds

**Evidence.**
- No event or cluster is in more than one fold (0 of 54,008 events, 0 of 19,460 clusters).
- `prep_data.py:87–115` lets each root claim only its own window. Inside the clustering's own
  (−0.25T, +T) windows of 13 M≥5 events, 92 events from other folds remain (3,681 records, all
  M ≤ 2.9). For M≥4 events the count is 229 events (6,352 records).
- The largest cases are the Jan 2017 Campotosto M5.0–5.5 events and the Aug 2016 Amatrice event
  (fold 0): 20–22 other-fold events each, more than 61 km from Norcia.
- 2,043 cross-fold event pairs lie within 10 km and 2 days. Four involve an M≥4, e.g. M4.3 /
  M4.4 events (cluster 55, fold 4) with M2.7 / M3.0 events 3–9 km and minutes to hours away
  (cluster 54, fold 3).
- One apparent cross-fold M5.3 inside an M4.9's window (25 Jan 2012, 29 km deep, fold 3, vs
  Emilia 29 May, fold 1) is a geometric overlap of distinct sequences, not leakage.

**Impact.** Arm-neutral and small-magnitude. "Completely unseen 2016–17 Central Italy sequence"
(README.md:40) is slightly too strong. **Fix.** Use chained or union-find clustering over
overlapping windows, or a spatio-temporal buffer between folds, and soften the wording.

### L4 (Low). Duplicate catalogue entries of the two largest Visso events

**Evidence.** Two Visso events are each listed twice, with very different magnitudes; both
copies are in fold 0:
- Mw 5.4 (`8663031`) also appears as ML 4.5 (`8663041`): 0.8 s apart, 39 shared station traces.
- Mw 5.9 (`8669321`) also appears as ML 4.5 (`8669361`): 1.5 s apart, 12 shared traces.

One trace on 22 Nov 2012 is shared across folds by two unrelated events (`dupev_out.json`).

**Impact.** About 140 records labelled 4.5 carry M5.4–5.9 waveforms. They enter the M≥4 test
metrics and penalize high predictions, which is conservative for the claims. They also add
conflicting labels to the training of folds 1–4, for all arms. **Fix.** Merge or drop the ML
duplicates.

### L5 (Low). The "standard split" is temporal, not sequence-held-out

**Evidence.** 51,839 of 96,993 VAL (2018) records, and 153 of its 1,027 M≥4 records, belong to
GK clusters that also contain training-period (2005–17) events, chiefly the Central Italy
aftershocks. For TEST the figures are 9% and 0 M≥4. **Impact.** The README describes it
correctly ("train 2005–17, no selection on VAL"), but it is not independent of sequences.

### L6 (Low). GEOFON prefixes are not strictly causal

**Evidence.** 78% of GEOFON records are 20 Hz. `geofon_prep.py:52–58, 79` cuts the 5 s window at
P and then applies `resample_poly`, a symmetric FIR with half-length 0.5 s at up = 5. So the
"1 s" and "3 s" prefixes contain filtered energy from up to about 0.1–0.5 s past the deadline.
**Impact.** Arm-neutral; it slightly flatters all GEOFON 1 s numbers. **Fix.** Resample the
full trace first and cut afterwards, or use a causal filter.

### L7 (Low). Frontier and bulk-capped comparisons tune the decision on the evaluation data

**Evidence.** `evaluate.py:242–252` picks the best r per bootstrap replicate on the same
records. Both arms get the same treatment and the README headline uses the fixed median.
**Impact.** The bulk-capped numbers in FINDINGS are oracle values; label them so.

### L8 (Low). The Chile picker uses post-pick data

**Evidence.** `chile_prep.py:52–78`: AIC refinement uses up to 1 s after the trigger, and picks
are accepted by post-pick SNR. **Impact.** Arm-neutral. Record selection and the deadline clock
use slightly more than d seconds.

## Checks that passed

1. **Fold integrity.** event→fold and cluster→fold are unique. `events.csv` and `meta.npz` agree
   on fold, cluster and magnitude for every record. Fold files of 9 main arms (mix, eqgr and
   eqdgr, seeds 1–3) cover all 1,159,249 records exactly once, each in its own fold. Chile and
   GEOFON have no cross-fold events or clusters.
2. **Labels and thresholds.** All INSTANCE labels are on the 0.1 grid; none lie in [3.9, 4.0).
   `y ≥ t − 1e-6` and the 3.95 cut for false M4 are consistent with 0.1 rounding. The tail-weight
   edge `T_EDGE` is 3.95.
3. **Input causality, INSTANCE.** The cache is raw counts `[P, P+500)`, cut at the catalogue
   pick (`fullmodeltrain/instancepipeline.py`, used by `extras/build_cache.py`). It has no
   filtering, only a global per-channel standardization; prefix demeaning is causal. float16
   per-record scaling by the 5 s peak puts no 1 s or 3 s prefix in the subnormal range (40k-record
   sample; median 1 s/5 s peak ratio .69), so quantization leaks nothing.
4. **Label-side metadata stays out of the inputs.** Distance and magnitude type are training
   labels only (`train.py:69–73, 101–104`); inference marginalizes them (`models.py:348–358`).
   The static features are an explicit allowlist with no identifiers or time
   (`instrument_inventory.model_features`). Chile's `p_after_network_s` input is zeroed
   (`chile_prep.py:145`).
5. **Normalization on training rows only** (`common.py:82–85`, called per fold at `train.py:57`),
   including after held-station removal.
6. **Same schedule for every arm.** `record.json` args differ only in head / seed / epochs /
   hold_stations as labelled; 10 epochs; same lr, batch, wd, k and seed per seed pair. The shared
   trunk and data code did not change while the arms were trained (`git diff 16bffd1..HEAD`
   adds only `width_mult` / `kappa_min` with identical defaults). Training times are comparable.
7. **eqgr pre-specification** confirmed by the commit and queue timestamps (M3).
8. **Headline numbers reproduce.** Medians recomputed from the saved PMFs without the project's
   evaluator match the JSONs (bulk / M≥4 / M≥5 MAE at 5 s):
   - mix .23833 / .53002 / .70288 and eqgr .23516 / .50403 / .54816 (`eval_final_all.json`:
     .2383 / .5300 / .7029 and .5482)
   - ensembles: mix .69828, eqgr .53662, eqdgr .49189 (reported .6983 / .5366 / .4919)
   - held stations: mix_hs .77087, eqdgr_hs .53771 (reported .7709 / .5377)
   - false-M4 rates (6.26 mix, 7.76 eqgr, 9.87 eqdgr ensemble, 29.24 mix_hs on held stations,
     per 10⁴) and event-level M≥5 MAE match as well.
9. **Ensembles average posteriors** (a mixture of PMFs) rather than point predictions
   (`evaluate.py:36–45`). The mean action is the mean of the mixture.
10. **Paired bootstrap.** Both arms use the same multinomial cluster weights
    (`evaluate.py:85, 229–238`). The unit is the GK cluster; event-level M≥4 MAE is computed per
    event, then averaged.
11. **NaN handling.** Every evaluated arm covers all records, so nothing is silently dropped.
    Sparse-tail replicates with no records are dropped and need at least 50 valid replicates
    (`geofon_eval.py:68–70`, `multistation.py:155–157`).
12. **Held-station construction** as in L2.
13. **Fusion priors.** `hist_adj` uses training-fold labels only. The calibrated tilt is
    cross-fitted across folds (`multistation.py:116–131`). `export_logpmf.py` reproduces the fold
    plan and static normalization.
14. **Chile and GEOFON specifics.** The Chile PMF grid reaches 9.5 against a maximum label of
    8.05, so predictions are never capped. GEOFON's 50 M≥7 events sit in 46 clusters, so its
    tail is not concentrated. Chile's MA-scored M≥6 gain is spread across all 8 clusters.

## What survives

**Robust on INSTANCE.**
- `eqgr` beats `mix_nll` on M≥4 error at every deadline with equal or better bulk: 194
  clusters, robust to LOCO and to the jackknife.
- On held stations, the same holds for M≥5 (`eqgr_hs`, jackknife CI excludes zero).
- The direction of the M≥5 gain holds in most sequences.

**Overstated.**
- The size and significance of the M≥5 gains: mostly one sequence (H1).
- eqdgr's false-alarm cost: 10× understated (H2).
- The Chile tail gain: label-dependent (M1).
- The sign-test p-values: dependent cells (M2).
- The fusion k-curves: duplicate sensors (M4).
- The post-hoc "best model" statements (M3).
