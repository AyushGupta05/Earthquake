math_core.py:110
The ULP of the final `mean` does not bound the error incurred computing `delta` and then adding it to `mu[k]`. When those terms nearly cancel, a tiny resulting mean has a tiny ULP even though the subtraction lost substantial precision relative to the posterior sigma. For example, an admitted prior centered at 512 with scale 1 and three factors having beta=1e6, sigma=1, and observed value=1 produces a posterior scale around 5.8e-7 and severe cancellation; its evidence ULP still passes this check. The frozen contract requires conditional-mean roundoff <=1e-10 times sigma. Propagate absolute arithmetic errors through precision, delta, and the final addition, and reject unresolved completion rather than using the result's ULP as an error certificate.
