No new model fit or benchmark-superiority claim follows from this audit. The targeted online search adds two primary-source checks to the existing literature review; it is not an exhaustive census of every publication.

## Thapa et al.: accessible preprint versus published preview

The [publisher preview](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10518-026-02403-1) establishes the February2026 paper and the broad hybrid neural/probabilistic claim. Full published methods remain behind subscription access. The [author preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7479899/v1), posted September2025, is a separate version:31pages, downloaded1359343bytes, SHA256 `075bd033605148d1af38bd3bb3b136c9f5a03943f5341c4950e8758ba888fce6`.

Visual checks of PDFpages12 and20 confirm a70/15/15 station-separated split and an evaluation table assigning different datasets to its methods. Table2 lists .21 MAE for the proposed approach on an authors’ dataset, while other rows name Hi-net, STEAD and INSTANCE. That table is not evidence of a matched comparison on our events or deadlines. The inspected text does not establish event isolation or separate1/3/5-second scores. Its displacement-energy accumulation and posterior sampling narrow novelty claims for neural features plus energy dynamics plus Bayesian inference. They do not justify copying an unspecified posterior likelihood or treating its score as our target benchmark. These observations concern the accessible preprint; changes in the published full text have not been verified.

## Glehman et al.: strong large-event result at a different clock

The [full2026 seismogeodetic paper](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2025JB033222) analyzes17 Mw7.0–9.1 earthquakes, uses acceleration/GNSS evidence and addresses fault-mechanism-dependent radiation effects. It reports approximately±.2 magnitude accuracy at2–3minutes. The authors explicitly distinguish this from seconds-scale near-source alerts. This supports multisensor and mechanism controls for large events, but not a matched1/3/5-second seismic benchmark. No new data acquisition or model fit was performed here.

## Consequences for our next experiments

A reusable architecture still needs the same event split, causal preprocessing, available input channels, magnitude scale and clock before its score can be compared. Station holdout alone does not guarantee event holdout: two records of one earthquake can be assigned to different station groups. This is a general evaluation constraint, not a claim that the inspected authors violated it.

The immediate data work remains justified: preserve original arrival clocks, establish usable compatible-Mw waveforms, then test a distributional method against retrained comparators on an independently held-out event/sequence panel. A short-window model cannot acquire GNSS or later energy evidence merely by changing a prior or sampling more posterior values.
