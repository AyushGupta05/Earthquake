# Tail correction with explicit bulk-risk constraints

11 October 2026. Primary-source and exact invented-arithmetic audit only: no actual predictions, labels, waveform arrays, new fits or paid resources. General risk-controlled post-processing is established prior art. This note identifies a useful comparator and a validation requirement, not a novel EEW method or a fitted result.

The user's objective has two separate parts: learn a better magnitude distribution from the available evidence, and choose an action whose tail improvement preserves ordinary error. A higher quantile can change the second part without improving the first. A smaller error on large earthquakes does not establish better distribution calibration, smaller bulk error or a physical source mechanism.

## Relevant primary work

**Learn then Test.** Angelopoulos and colleagues test a fixed family of post-processing choices using held-out calibration data and multiple testing. Their Section 2.4 combines several risk constraints through the largest valid component p-value; their theory also permits no admissible choice. Section 3.3 treats selective regression. This supplies a general testing comparator rather than EEW-specific tail information. Its calibration assumptions must be checked at the event/sequence level; repeated records of the same earthquake are not independent calibration units. Mean-risk tests cannot be substituted directly for median absolute error. [Primary full text, arXiv:2110.01052v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.01052v5). The version header is 29 September 2022; the generated HTML body displays 24 August 2026, so that body date is not used to claim a new paper.

**Non-monotone conformal risk control.** Angelopoulos's February 2026 preprint extends risk-control reasoning through algorithmic stability, including multidimensional parameters and multi-group debiasing. Its principal guarantee is in expectation, under the stated stability/exchangeability assumptions. That differs from a high-probability guarantee that each selected correction preserves each bulk and tail metric. A bootstrap estimate of stability is not an exact certificate of those assumptions. [Primary full text, arXiv:2602.20151v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20151v1). The publisher search result was available, but opening the DOI failed; this note relies on the preprint, not an assumed identical journal edition.

**Risk-Controlled Post-Processing of Decision Policies.** Joshi, Wang, Hassani and Dobriban's May 2026 preprint explicitly retains a baseline except where a fitted score favors switching to a fallback. It maximizes baseline agreement subject to a chance constraint. Its general fitted-fallback guarantees require regularity and i.i.d. assumptions; the stronger exact-safe fallback regime requires an action that is safe for every possible label. This is very close prior art for a distribution-derived selective correction. It does not establish simultaneous EEW MAE, MedAE and large-magnitude improvements. A magnitude point action usually cannot be exact-safe under symmetric absolute error over a broad magnitude support: changing it improves some possible labels and worsens others. [Primary full text, arXiv:2605.06479v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06479v1), Sections 3–4. Version header 7 May 2026; the same generated-body date discrepancy is retained.

**EEW decision context.** Cremen and Galasso's 2021 primary repository abstract describes end-user preferences, uncertain earthquake scenarios and alert/mitigation decisions. It supports the distinction between prediction and downstream decisions, not a magnitude-error improvement at 1/3/5 seconds. Only its primary abstract and publication record were read here. [UCL repository record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10126046/).

## Exact counterexample: a tail lift can worsen ordinary error

Suppose the available prefix carries no information, with magnitude 2 occurring with probability .98 and magnitude 7 with probability .02. The calibrated absolute-error action is the median, 2. Its overall MAE is .1; its high-magnitude error is 5. Raising every action to 2.1 lowers high-magnitude error to 4.9 but raises overall MAE to .196. The exact differences are +.096 overall and −.1 on the large event. The unchanged input entropy cannot distinguish which event should receive the lift.

This is an invented finite-population calculation, independently checked with exact rational arithmetic in [the saved arithmetic](../work/earthquake_risk_constraint_audit/invented_arithmetic.json). It is not an EEW result or a universal impossibility claim. It shows why a distribution-only upward shift cannot promise both objectives without informative conditioning or an explicitly allowed tradeoff. A correctly calibrated unique median already minimizes conditional absolute risk; a nonzero action change cannot universally improve that same risk.

For a predetermined correction bounded by `|a_new − a_base| ≤ d`, the triangle inequality gives the paired absolute-error difference in `[-d,d]`. Thus a test can directly address excess MAE rather than testing each model's absolute error separately. At `d=.1`, a deliberately conservative one-sided Hoeffding upper bound, with a union bound over 9 fixed candidates and 6 mean-risk constraints, gives radius

`2d × sqrt(log(54/.05)/(2n))`.

It is approximately .0682/.0374/.0118/.00483 for hypothetical independent-event counts 30/100/1,000/6,000. Radius .002 would require 34,924 under this particular bound. These are not actual cohort counts, a power lower bound, a required sample size or a proposed acceptance gate. Variance-aware paired methods can be tighter; dependent sequences or adaptive candidate creation require additional treatment. The calculation makes the limited strength of small-tail certificates explicit. It does not certify MedAE, CVaR, unbounded NLL or transport to another region.

## A defensible later comparator

On a new frozen event/sequence split, fit any candidate fallback and its score using TRAIN only. It could use the full prefix distribution plus new verified information; entropy alone is only one matched control. Freeze the finite correction family before a separate calibration set is read. Include identity, a fixed upward quantile, a score-blind mixture, a learned residual correction and a distribution-derived conditional-risk score at the same information and capacity budget.

Calibrate paired excess bulk MAE at each of 1/3/5 seconds, rather than hiding a regression by averaging deadlines. Tail MAE, MedAE and worst-error/proper-score measures need their own predeclared assessments and sufficiently supported independent evaluation. Keep the original distribution for NLL/CRPS when testing only an action change; an improved point action must not be presented as an improved probability model. If no correction qualifies, retain the baseline and the negative result. A threshold learned on the already repeatedly inspected validation set would remain exploratory.

**Decision:** preserve risk-controlled post-processing as a known-method comparator. No fit is earned by this audit, and the broad wrapper is not a novel contribution. The current independent large-event data and strict-clock availability checks remain the immediate prerequisites. A future contribution would need an identifiable additional early measurement or a specific observation-law construction, then gains against these same-input controls on independent large events.
