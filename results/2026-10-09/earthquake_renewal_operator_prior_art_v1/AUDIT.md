# Receiver-activity renewal: bounded novelty audit

12 October 2026. Public-source advisory only. No private event rows, waveform bodies, fitted predictions, clocks, inventories or credentials were read. No code, data requests, fits, AWS resources or Git state were changed. The reviewed project documents were `earthquake_tail_innovation_candidates_v2/DECISION.md`, its `PROTOCOL.md`, and `outputs/earthquake_method_status.md`.

**Decision: retain one narrow experimental hypothesis, reject a broad novelty claim.** An observed active/quiet process with run-age-dependent transition probabilities is a conventional duration-dependent state model. Right-censored stays, recurrent episodes and Bayesian magnitude normalization are established ingredients. The bounded search did not locate an exact local-receiver, nonabsorbing, magnitude-conditioned run-age likelihood tested against the proposed matched controls at fixed 1/3/5-second network deadlines. That absence is not evidence of novelty. A useful, independently replicated application could be a contribution; a new probability principle is not established.

## Exact surviving question

The proposed C operator compresses each newly available quarter-second cell into a receiver activity state, scores observed stays/exits/reactivations conditional on Mw and strictly earlier information, and normalizes one Mw density. It may encode episode-age structure more efficiently with little TRAIN data than a full-prefix CNN or direct activity features. It supplies no extra information unavailable to those baselines. Its strongest clean contrast remains C versus the age-free H model on identical observations, losses and initialization; competition against full-prefix B and direct-feature F establishes usefulness rather than identifying only the age mechanism.

The existing protocol's H logits also depend on elapsed local time. H is therefore age-free, but its episode-duration law is not generally a stationary geometric distribution. Use “age-free conditional hazard” in a scientific description. Quiet-to-active reactivation is important for the observable and censoring bookkeeping; it is not independently novel.

## One small modification worth preregistering, conditionally

**Allow magnitude sensitivity to depend on current run age.** If the original C pilot earns the TRAIN variation/support gates, a separate protocol could compare C against one nested extension Cx:

`ell_s_Cx = ell_s_C + kappa_s * (m - mref) * log(1 + r_previous)`, for `s = on, off`.

This adds only two coefficients. For fixed context/time/state, C has a constant transition log-odds contrast between magnitudes: `Delta_m * beta_s`. Cx permits `Delta_m * (beta_s + kappa_s * log(1+r_previous))`. The restriction concerns the transition log-odds contrast; C already has time-varying hazard probabilities, informative censored stays and time-evolving posterior densities. It is not generally wrong without the interaction.

**Motivation is an inference, not a published result:** Trugman's time-dependent saturation and Münchmeyer's delayed separation caution against assuming a constant magnitude effect across every observed episode age. A low-rank interaction might let early receiver activity remain weak evidence while longer sustained activity becomes informative. The cited source-rupture results do not identify the shape or sign of receiver hazards. Both coefficients stay unrestricted; no forced tail lift, monotonic high-M prior, source-duration law or new tail weighting is added. The statistical operation itself is conventional nonproportional duration modeling, as King and Weiss make explicit.

Use exactly the frozen activity extraction, original network deadlines, same initial density, objective, event orders, seeds and checkpoints as C. Cx is initialized with both kappas zero; C is exactly nested. Two coefficients will ordinarily fit within the existing 5% capacity tolerance, but root must verify actual counts before authorizing it. Keep the original C/H/B/F comparison intact. If adding another control, allocate the same two effective coefficients to initial-feature interactions in C, rather than padding with unused parameters. This is one prospectively frozen contrast, not an age-function, threshold or loss grid. No change is made to the current frozen protocol by this advisory.

Cx has no theorem guaranteeing unchanged bulk error. Keep the current all-duration high-M gain and zero-margin bulk/worst-error/probability-score acceptance criteria, with negative outcomes retained. Repeated reuse of the small stratified DEV cohort cannot establish natural-population bulk preservation. An apparent interaction gain must survive larger sealed, sequence-aware independent evaluation before any “tested new method” claim.

## What would reject the contribution

- Insufficient TRAIN transitions/ages or all active runs censored: do not fit a duration operator under the current protocol.
- C does not beat age-free H: no run-age mechanism result; any gain may be feature or objective value.
- C/Cx does not beat B/F while preserving bulk and worst errors at every deadline: no practical contribution for the user's objective.
- Gains vanish under grouped/external evidence or clock/metadata controls: no independent generalization result.
